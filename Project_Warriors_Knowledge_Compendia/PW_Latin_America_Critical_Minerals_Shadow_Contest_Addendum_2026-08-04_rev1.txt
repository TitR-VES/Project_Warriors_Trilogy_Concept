2. PREWAR LOCK

During the early Crodyn administration, Spook is heavily involved in CIA / Ground Branch black operations against cartel-controlled illegal mining, rare-earth extraction, and associated critical-mineral networks in Mexico, Guatemala, Colombia, and Brazil.

The operations form part of a wider American attempt to secure access, leverage, and practical control over strategically important mineral supply during the escalating technology competition with China.

The policy is not merely anti-crime. It combines covert action, corporate pressure, selective enforcement, political influence, local-security relationships, and deniable coercion. Compliant actors may receive protection, equipment, intelligence, commercial access, or political favors in exchange for alignment with American strategic and corporate priorities.

3. CARTEL CONSEQUENCE

Many cartel organizations develop durable hostility toward Americans associated with the CIA, Ground Branch, special operations, federal law enforcement, or corporate security.

Their grievance includes destroyed extraction capacity, lost revenue, compromised routes, recruited informants, support to rivals, political pressure, and American corporate access following covert disruption.

The criminal landscape remains fragmented rather than monolithic. Different organizations may oppose, bargain with, sell to, or temporarily cooperate with American actors.

4. LOS SIETES DISTINCTION

Los Sietes has a more transactional and less openly destructive history with American shadow and corporate power than many peer organizations.

It receives combinations of equipment, intelligence access, political protection, selective law-enforcement restraint, commercial introductions, logistical favors, and tolerated operating space. In exchange, it can provide deniable violence, regional access, smuggling capacity, source networks, specialist personnel, and intelligence-adjacent planning.

This explains:

- why Goldberg can contract Los Sietes for the Desautels Resort Hotel bombing, Operation HOLE PUNCH, and other sensitive deniable work;
- why Spook can later collaborate with Los Sietes when interests overlap;
- why the relationship remains professional, wary, and morally compromised rather than friendly or loyal.

Canonical spelling: LOS SIETES.

5. POSTWAR REVERSAL

After PANDORA, World War III, occupation, and American geopolitical contraction, the United States can no longer sustain its former regional protection and influence architecture.

China expands into the gap through reconstruction finance, infrastructure, mining and processing contracts, logistics investment, security and surveillance assistance, intelligence ties, corporate partnerships backed by state power, and political guarantees.

China may co-opt useful cartels, split or subordinate them, turn leadership through finance and intelligence pressure, or support their elimination when they obstruct stable resource access.

Former American partners and governments reassess alignment according to which external power can still provide money, security, market access, and political survival.

6. POSTWAR COVERT MICROCLIMATE

Latin America becomes an uneven arena of low-visibility competition among:

- Chinese intelligence and state-backed commercial-security networks;
- Chinese-aligned governments, businesses, security services, and criminal intermediaries;
- remnants of American intelligence and corporate operations;
- local actors attempting to hedge, defect, resist, or profit;
- cartel remnants seeking patrons or resisting subordination.

Typical conflict forms include source recruitment, sabotage, deniable killings, mine and port competition, data theft, financial pressure, proxy security operations, political influence, and extraction of technical or political personnel.

Residual American operations remain capable but no longer enjoy uncontested primacy.
