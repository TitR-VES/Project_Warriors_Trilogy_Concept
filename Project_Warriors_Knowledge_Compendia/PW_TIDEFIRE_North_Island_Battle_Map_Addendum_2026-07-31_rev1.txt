TIDEFIRE is Romeo's Mission 14 and the finale of Lead the Way.

The owner-supplied thirteen-placemark KMZ establishes a viable east-to-west
mission spine across the Coronado / North Island battlespace:

Coronado Bridge
-> NASNI gate and adjacent Coronado defense belt
-> Base-Defense Command Post
-> Tower Command

-> southwest range-sector fire-control handoff

-> arriving Humvee pickup
-> direct transport to the NCTS-hosted emergency operations closeout

- The pre-gate houses are in adjacent evacuated Coronado neighborhoods, not a
  civilian district “on North Island.”
- “Naval Facilities Command” is normalized to a fictionalized NASNI Base-Defense
  Command Post.
- Tower remains the air-picture and tasking node.
- The southeast hostile boat remains offshore; the land point is the player
  observation / engagement position.
- The small-arms-range handoff moves to the southwest Pacific-facing range sector.
- The NMESIS designation objective moves south to the true Zuñiga Point sector,
  immediately landward of the exposed jetty.
- The southwestern damage marker is North Island / Zuñiga-sector damage, not Point
  Loma damage.
- NCTS is the final emergency communications / operations cell, not automatically
  the air station's senior headquarters.

FLOW LOCKS

1. Bridge crossing is vehicle-led and already under threat.
2. Base Defense gives the ground and damage-control picture.
3. Tower gives the air picture and identifies the UAV-guidance element.
4. Delta destroys the boat or boat-supported guidance team with credible armored
   direct fire.
5. The UAV objective reduces attack accuracy and tempo but does not remove all UAVs.
6. A southwest littoral fire-control team hands Delta the NMESIS targeting task.
7. Delta defends the Zuñiga observation position while identifying and tracking the
   correct hostile vessels.
8. NMESIS mission-kills one high-value vessel and breaks the formation.
9. Alert aircraft launch from the surviving airfield route and exploit the window.
10. Delta begins the return from Zuñiga on foot and moves northeast only a short
    distance.
11. An arriving Humvee picks up the full team and takes it directly to the NCTS
    emergency cell. The player observes the ride but does not drive.
12. During transit, the player sees reduced UAV accuracy, lighter naval fire,
    alert aircraft airborne, and defenders regaining freedom of movement.
13. Delta reports that the immediate naval assault has lost momentum.

FINALE DESIGN RULE

The KMZ is the macro objective skeleton. The mission must add combat pressure
between nodes: bridge exposure, gate/perimeter fighting, hangar/service-road
clearance, the UAV-guidance engagement, a range-sector handoff under pressure,
and a defended Zuñiga designation sequence. TIDEFIRE cannot play as a sequence of
briefings and errands.

The final return is not an optional generic compression. Delta walks a short
northeast leg from Zuñiga, is collected by an arriving Humvee, and rides directly
to NCTS. This prevents a redundant full backtrack while preserving enough time for
the player to see the operational consequences of the strike.

The mission's operational climax is the NMESIS strike. The alert-fighter launch is
the visible payoff. The Humvee ride is the consequence-viewing transition. The
NCTS report is the emotional close. Romeo's unit creates the window through which
larger American forces act; Delta does not single-handedly end the Battle of
Coronado.
