4. CHARACTER PROMO FORMAT
Default seven-part structure:
1. origin;
2. first detonation/weapon moment;
3. character-specific tool or weapon moment;
4. deeper action;
5. quieter squad-relationship passage;
6. final build;
7. private character detail/title card.

Use four diegetic sound punctuations as a recurring series technique: music partially drops, a real-world sound cuts through, music returns. Each punctuation must reveal character rather than merely add impact.

Private weapon names may function as close-inspection Easter eggs, but current weapon sources control which names remain active and how they are presented.

5. SOUND-DESIGN PRINCIPLES
- Productive dissonance over literal mood matching. Warmth against wrongness is the established model.
- Silence is an authored asset. KYSC's missing normal chatter is part of their identity.
- Audio, HUD, and mechanics must agree: a HOPLON blackout should change player information access at the same moment it is announced.
- Shoot-and-scoot fire-support language must preserve displacement urgency.
- APOLLO-style monitoring gains force through long calm stretches followed by sudden escalation.
- Rare or culturally specific chatter loses power when spammed; pool management is part of writing.

Voice anchors
- Frost: sincere zero-inflection deadpan; no self-aware wink.
- Switchblade medic calls: specific location/injury/need, never generic "he's hit" filler.
- Romeo: warmer downtime voice; lower, slower contact register.
- Tripper's "Spook, I have found something and you are not going to like it" is semi-scripted and gains callback weight through limited reuse.
- Smoke's single-word "Spook" callout is calm and present; team response supplies the alarm.
