PURPOSE
Define reusable terms for describing how playable-character storylines relate across campaigns, missions, locations, events, and consequences. This page is the prose authority behind the additive narrative-structure ontology's touchpoint classifications.

SOURCE

CORE DISTINCTION
Classify the relationship by causal, relational, spatial, temporal, and structural significance rather than by physical proximity alone.

================================================================================
01. CORE TAXONOMY
================================================================================

INTERSECTION
Two or more playable-character storylines become directly connected through a narratively meaningful interaction, exchange, confrontation, collaboration, or dependency.

The characters may meet in person, communicate remotely, act through intermediaries, or operate as allies or enemies. The defining feature is that each storyline meaningfully enters the other.

Example:
Spook meets Sakhalin at Tomakomai Harbor and receives intelligence required to enter China through Yunnan and pursue the extraction of Y. T. Lin.

OVERLAP
Two or more playable-character storylines occupy the same physical or virtual space, operational window, or local event without necessarily interacting or becoming causally dependent.

Example:
Nick Hawthorne and Romeo are both present at Rollin' Good Times during separate assignments. Hawthorne is part of Sunny's security detail; Romeo is gathering intelligence on NAF capacity. Their presence overlaps even if they do not directly engage.

PARALLELISM
Two or more playable storylines run alongside one another as separate perspectives on the same broader operation, campaign phase, or event structure.

Example:
Ducky/AEGIS and Romeo/Fire Team Delta execute separate playable arms of the Hindeloopen operation. Each mission is independently meaningful and contributes to the shared result.

================================================================================
02. ADDITIONAL TERMS
================================================================================

CONVERGENCE
Previously distinct storyline paths are brought into sustained common purpose, shared conflict, or one combined end state. Convergence is stronger and more durable than a single intersection.

HANDOFF
One storyline passes information, an asset, responsibility, access, or consequence into another storyline. The characters may or may not meet.

CAUSAL LINKAGE
An action in one storyline directly creates, changes, enables, blocks, or escalates conditions in another storyline.

AFTERMATH ENCOUNTER
A later playable character enters the physical or narrative aftermath of another protagonist's earlier actions, recognizing or discovering the residue without necessarily meeting that protagonist.

COUNTERPOINT
Two storylines are deliberately juxtaposed to create thematic contrast, moral comparison, or different interpretations of similar circumstances. They need not share a place or direct causal link.

COLLISION
Two storylines meet through direct conflict, incompatible objectives, or adversarial action. Collision is a hostile or destabilizing form of intersection.

NEAR MISS
Two protagonists pass close to one another in place, time, or information space without recognizing the significance or making contact. A near miss can be a specialized overlap.

SYNCHRONIZATION POINT
A major shared event anchors multiple campaigns relative to one another in chronology. The protagonists do not need to interact or be co-located.

Example:
Crodyn's wartime address is experienced from different locations across campaigns and fixes their relative timeline positions.

SHARED EVENT
Two or more campaigns experience the same event, but the event may not be strong enough to function as the primary chronological anchor.

BRANCH-DEPENDENT CONVERGENCE
Storylines converge only if a prior player choice, survival state, optional discovery, or campaign flag is satisfied. The project should preserve the conditional state rather than silently selecting one global branch.

================================================================================
03. RECOMMENDED HIERARCHY
================================================================================

Umbrella category:
STORYLINE RELATIONSHIP / TOUCHPOINT

Spatial or temporal relationship:
- Overlap
- Near Miss
- Synchronization Point
- Shared Event

Event or structural relationship:
- Parallelism
- Counterpoint
- Causal Linkage
- Aftermath Encounter

Direct narrative connection:
- Intersection
- Handoff
- Collision

Sustained unification:
- Convergence
- Branch-Dependent Convergence

Useful increasing-connection shorthand:
PARALLELISM -> OVERLAP -> INTERSECTION -> CONVERGENCE

This shorthand is not a strict sequence every storyline must follow. Handoff, collision, causal linkage, counterpoint, and aftermath encounter describe specific shapes that may occur at any stage.

================================================================================
04. ANNOTATION RULES
================================================================================

- A single touchpoint may carry more than one classification when each adds distinct information.
- Prefer the most specific term that explains why the relationship matters.
- Do not label simple chronology adjacency as an intersection.
- Do not label co-location as convergence unless the storylines actually unify.
- Use synchronization point for chronology control, not merely because two missions occur on the same date.
- Preserve branch conditions explicitly.
- Record campaign and installment references separately from the relationship term.
- Physical meeting is neither necessary nor sufficient for intersection.
