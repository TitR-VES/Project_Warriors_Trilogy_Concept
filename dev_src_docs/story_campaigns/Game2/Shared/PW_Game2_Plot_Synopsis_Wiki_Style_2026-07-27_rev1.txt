PLOT
===============================================================================

Project Warriors II follows the war after the immediate PANDORA crisis has hardened into occupation, counteroffensive, and institutional fracture. The United States remains divided between surviving military authorities, occupied territories, local resistance networks, and the wartime government of President Barry James Crodyn. Europe is still fighting a conventional Russian offensive while also confronting a covert technical campaign that has penetrated civilian infrastructure and allied command systems. At the same time, the conspiracy uncovered by Victor Two Bravo in the first game has survived exposure by destroying evidence, manipulating the official record, and allowing its principal architects to disappear behind the larger war.

The three campaigns again depict different layers of the same conflict. Ramón “Romeo” Álvarez enters an occupied American community and helps transform local survival networks into the American Freedom Corps. Ferris “Ducky” Ryan and AEGIS Squad move from defending allied infrastructure to identifying and dismantling a repeatable Russian relay-and-deception architecture built with compromised knowledge of the system they protect. Trevor “Spook” Gartner, operating outside a trustworthy chain of command, follows evidence that Edward Goldberg is still alive while trying to preserve witnesses and records that powerful institutions want erased.

Crodyn's wartime presidential address serves as the principal synchronization point between the campaigns. In Kentucky, the speech publicly designates Romeo and the American Freedom Corps as enemies of wartime order. In the wider military and intelligence stories, it demonstrates that the political system created by PANDORA is no longer merely failing to respond to the crisis; it is actively defining resistance, disclosure, and independent action as threats to its own authority.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AMERICAN FREEDOM
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The first campaign follows Staff Sergeant Ramón “Romeo” Álvarez after the Battle of Coronado. Although Romeo ends the first game as a Ranger still operating inside the remnants of the American military chain of command, he does not immediately become a resistance leader. His route from Southern California to Kentucky is intended to show the gradual collapse of institutional legitimacy and the personal dislocation that separates him from the force structure in which he began the war.

[DEVELOPMENT GAP — POST-CORONADO BRIDGE] The exact duration, route, intermediate locations, unit status, and circumstances that carry Romeo from Coronado to Calloway have not yet been fully developed. The landed source establishes the function of the bridge—soldiering becoming local responsibility—but not a complete playable sequence.

Romeo reaches Calloway, Kentucky, approximately one month into the local occupation. The campaign opens on a familiar American town made wrong through restraint rather than spectacle: foreign checkpoints stand among pharmacies, civic buildings, damaged houses, churches, schools, and ordinary roads. Romeo initially observes the community from its edge instead of arriving as a liberating hero. He is introduced to a loose resistance network whose strength comes less from weapons than from routes, medicine, records, radios, local knowledge, and people willing to keep one another alive.

The early network is anchored by pharmacist Dale Pruett, former school superintendent Renata Voss, volunteer firefighter and mechanic Cody Beaumont, former 82nd Airborne staff sergeant Frank Kowalski, and delivery driver Gene Marlow. Each represents a different kind of authority that survived the occupation. Dale provides civic and medical logistics, Renata maintains information and communications, Cody supplies route knowledge and urgency, Frank gives the cell practical military counsel, and Marlow uses his work and familiarity with checkpoints to move people and supplies.

Romeo's first operations are reconnaissance and preparation rather than open revolt. With Cody, he surveys the occupation from elevated ground, identifies garrison positions, signals equipment, detainee transports, and a concealed culvert route, and observes the unmarked Russian intelligence officer Glushkin interrogating Marlow. Romeo then disrupts the occupation's communications long enough to contact surviving command personnel and learn that Calloway is expected to become more than an isolated cell. A pre-positioned cache southeast of town is meant to help establish the first node of a wider insurgent network.

The discovery of civilians detained inside the medical-clinic parking structure turns preparation into an immediate moral problem. Cody recognizes Dale's daughter Kelsey among the prisoners but does not compromise the reconnaissance. Romeo and the cell recover the hidden weapons and medical cache through Marlow's farm-road route, then launch the rescue operation known as “Second Level.” Romeo clears the lower structure while Cody holds the withdrawal route long enough for the detainees to escape through the culvert.

Cody remains behind too long and is wounded and captured after securing the civilians' escape. His loss is not treated as a triumphant martyrdom. In the aftermath, Renata asks Romeo whether another version of the plan existed in which Cody came home. Romeo receives no easy absolution, and Frank's answer is that the cost must be made to mean something. The exchange establishes the campaign's central tension: Romeo is capable of organizing effective violence, but leadership requires him to remain accountable for the lives spent making that violence possible.

As occupation pressure increases, the Calloway cell begins connecting with neighboring communities. Romeo encounters Patrice Odom's emergency-management network, Terrell Washington's Cumberland community, Judge Hargrove's documentary and legitimacy lane, Ray Braddock's damaged state-trooper network, and Priya Okonkwo's independent Kentucky National Guard veterans. The campaign emphasizes that these groups are not waiting to be absorbed into Romeo's personal army. Each has its own people, territory, risk tolerance, and moral authority. Romeo earns cooperation by giving honest assessments of danger and by accepting that some groups cannot be used safely simply because they are willing to fight.

Fire Team Delta eventually reunites with Romeo, restoring the operational rhythm between Romeo, Dingo, Howler, and Beaver while changing the context in which that team now operates. Their military experience improves the resistance's ability to interdict occupation logistics and coordinate multiple cells, but the Rangers do not replace the local network that sustained Calloway before they arrived.

[DEVELOPMENT GAP — MIDDLE-LADDER ORDER] Two substantial clusters of middle-campaign missions are source-landed but have not been reconciled into one contradiction-free order. One cluster centers on the Fire Team Delta reunion, counter-mapping Glushkin, Priya's unit, and Operation AMBITIOUS. The other centers on county-to-county network formation, public legitimacy, and the campaign's presidential-address hinge. The synopsis therefore presents these developments thematically rather than assigning definitive mission numbers.

Glushkin emerges as Romeo's principal occupation antagonist. Rather than merely hunting whoever fires on Russian patrols, he studies relationships, routes, communications habits, and social obligations. He attempts to turn local trust into a map of the resistance. When an insecure Middlesboro group creates an exploitable seam, Romeo's network is forced to close the leak while accepting that it cannot save everyone cleanly. The conflict becomes a contest between two forms of organization: Glushkin's professional counter-network pressure and the resistance's attempt to remain distributed without becoming incoherent.

The American Freedom Corps name arises from use rather than branding. During the first coordinated multi-cell operations, Terrell proposes “American Freedom something,” and Romeo completes the name as American Freedom Corps. The AFC subsequently attacks the occupation's county logistics system, intercepts monitoring equipment, disrupts communications, and moves documentary evidence of occupation abuses through civilian routes to outside media. Judge Hargrove's involvement reinforces the idea that the movement must remain something its members could defend publicly and legally, not merely something capable of winning firefights.

The AFC's growing visibility eventually reaches surviving conventional American forces. After a significant garrison action and contact with a 101st Airborne element, reports of Romeo's network reach Crodyn's government. In his wartime address, the president names Romeo and the AFC as enemies of order. The resistance responds without a celebratory rally. “American Freedom” is repeated as a statement of identity: the government has attempted to turn the phrase into an accusation, while the people in the room understand it as a description of what they are trying to preserve.

The recovered Calloway arc culminates in “Free Ground,” a coordinated attack on multiple occupation positions. The resistance temporarily retakes the Elks Lodge, where Aldridge raises the folded American flag preserved by Patrice Odom. The occupation later retakes the building, but the point of the operation cannot be undone. The public has seen the flag fly over the town again, and the AFC has demonstrated that resistance can become a visible civic fact even when it cannot yet hold every position it takes.

[DEVELOPMENT GAP — CAMPAIGN CONTINUATION AND FINALE] “Free Ground” is the climax of the source-landed Calloway formation arc, not a confirmed finale for the entire American Freedom campaign. The exact placement of Fire Team Delta's reunion, the optional “Face to Face” Glushkin mission, the handoff to conventional American forces, the wider counteroffensive beyond Kentucky, and the campaign's final operation remain open. The exact calendar placement of Crodyn's address under the November 7, 2028 election anchor also requires reconciliation.

PERSISTENT THREAT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The second campaign follows Sergeant First Class Ferris “Ducky” Ryan and AEGIS Squad as the European war changes from the defense of a threatened system into an offensive investigation of how that system has been compromised. Ducky continues to operate alongside Finnish sniper and arctic-warfare specialist Aki “Frost” Peltola, Romanian assault specialist Ştefan “Lupin” Dragavei, and Australian combat medic Cassandra “Switchblade” Stewart. Analyst Tara Halliday remains the campaign's principal institutional counterpart, preserving anomalies and records that others would prefer to treat as isolated incidents.

Persistent Threat is built around the discovery that the enemy is not simply attacking European infrastructure from outside. Russian technical and intelligence elements are using knowledge of AEGIS-adjacent architecture to hide relay, certification, deception, and access systems inside the ordinary mechanisms through which Europe already functions. The ORBITAL DECAY attack at Torrejón also demonstrates that compromised access can be exploited by unmarked Chinese operators, broadening the operational problem without requiring every hostile action to belong to one unified command. Commercial logistics, temporary installations, contractor credentials, exhibition facilities, industrial service routes, power, cooling, fiber, precision manufacturing, and protected civilian movement become part of the battlefield.

The opening mission, “Failure Cascade,” retains the Norwegian coastal withdrawal previously developed under the shorthand “Cold Open.” AEGIS fights through a Russian naval-infantry landing, links up with Norwegian coastal defenders, and extracts after Switchblade takes a controlled risk to save an elderly civilian. Walker's message after exfil—“Good exfil. We have a problem.”—opens the investigation.

“Dead Frequency” takes place at a converted NATO facility in northern Estonia. After completing the primary objective, AEGIS withdraws under closing pursuit while Swedish Archer artillery under the ARTEMIS callsign shapes the enemy's approach routes. Lupin photographs technical parameters showing that Russian personnel possess internal AEGIS nomenclature, creating Investigative Beat 1, “The Parameters,” and confirming insider-level access.

In “Orbital Decay,” unmarked Chinese operators penetrate the NATO/AEGIS satellite-command layer at Torrejón de Ardoz and attempt to force the constellation out of orbit. AEGIS and local defenders interrupt the full sequence, but one satellite falls toward Slovenia. “Splashdown” follows immediately in the Julian Alps as AEGIS works with Slovenian special-operations, civil-defense, resistance, and technical-recovery partners to secure the wreckage before hostile teams can exploit it. The paired missions transform the compromise from a Russian intelligence anomaly into a broader operational vulnerability. Recovered fragments of a wider target and movement package warn Halliday that a high-value lithography transfer in the Netherlands is about to be attacked.

“Process Window,” a working title, is the restored Game 2 ASML operation at a fictionalized high-security technology campus and integration complex in Veldhoven. A deniable Chinese-directed sabotage cell uses compromised contractor access, commercial drones, roof teams, and technical-action personnel to target the combined recovery chain needed for advanced semiconductor production: a critical lithography module, calibration and qualification data, specialist tooling, controlled technical infrastructure, and the engineers capable of restoring it. The mission avoids treating the semiconductor industry as one magical machine; the attackers need only destroy the irreplaceable combination to make Western recovery nonviable.

Ducky remains the mission's primary playable and tactical perspective. When hostile roof teams and an anti-vehicle element pin the moving technical convoy, control shifts briefly and diegetically to Lupin on an elevated campus position. His SCAR-H designated-marksman section is shorter, closer-ranged, and more urgent than Frost's precision grammar: the player rapidly prioritizes drone operators, marksmen, breach personnel, and moving vehicle threats while visibly protecting Ducky, Switchblade, Dutch defenders, engineers, and convoy vehicles below. Control returns to Ducky as soon as Lupin reopens the route.

AEGIS and its Dutch partners preserve the critical module, qualification archive, and essential engineering team, but secondary tooling, support infrastructure, and service capacity are damaged. The outcome is therefore a costly partial victory: a viable Western recovery path survives, while the semiconductor bottleneck that shapes Game 3 remains intact. Contractor-routing and credential evidence recovered from the sabotage cell points Halliday toward a mobile EW and logistics convoy in Latvia.

“Hard Winter” tracks that mobile EW convoy across the Latvian interior over two in-game days. Its aftermath remains Investigative Beat 2, “The Architecture”: Halliday follows AEGIS development contracts through Nexus Meridian Holdings and the KINGMAKER-connected financial infrastructure already visible in the Montoya Farm records. Process Window supplies the operational lead but does not replace Hard Winter's authored discovery.

Hard Winter leads into the three-mission Bakhmut mini-arc. In “Dead Ground,” AEGIS links up with a Ukrainian veteran cell led by Captain Oleksandr Honcharenko and begins recognizing the geometry of a protected technical site inside the ruined city. Frost temporarily becomes playable to clear controller and overwatch positions whose survival would compromise the route.

In “Dark Fiber,” the combined force strips an outer dependency from the target system and exploits buried communications and service infrastructure. A temporary shift to Lupin places the player in a faster interdiction role while Ducky continues directing the larger tactical problem. The recovered material establishes that the site is not merely a local relay, but a forward certification facility used to test attack logic against AEGIS-shaped defensive interpretation.

The arc concludes with “Ash Relay,” a Ducky-led assault on the municipal communications-and-utilities complex. The team destroys the relay architecture in the required order, seizes the surviving technical packet, and escapes before the outer screen can close. The evidence converts Halliday's suspicions into physical proof of a repeatable relay-and-deception kill chain and identifies Svalbard as the probable northern target.

“Open Circuit” follows Ash Relay as the final time-critical continental exploitation and denial mission before AEGIS deploys north. At the service side of the Brno Exhibition Centre, AEGIS and the attached SHIELD exploitation team discover that the visible technical floor is a decoy and that the real node is concealed along a protected service artery. SHIELD removes the routing core while French Rafale support under the KERAUNOS callsign breaks the final vehicle-and-overwatch choke point. The mission demonstrates that the same technical war visible in Bakhmut is also hidden inside ordinary European commercial systems. Its placement does not replace Bakhmut's Svalbard reveal.

AEGIS returns to Svalbard in “Permafrost,” where the northern strike package threatens the infrastructure that survived the first game's opening crisis. After the relevant node is destroyed, Norwegian K239 Chunmoo rocket artillery under the TYPHON callsign collapses inland pursuit routes and creates a temporary coastal extraction window. Lupin suffers a shoulder fragment wound during the operation before the team reaches a rigid-inflatable craft under fire.

In “Hull Breach,” AEGIS and Nereid Black infiltrate Kaliningrad harbor aboard the USS District of Columbia and assault the Russian intelligence vessel Akademik Volkov. Lupin handles the intelligence exploitation despite his Permafrost wound. The recovered drive contains Tremblay's ZEUS-level credential identifier and biometric profile, creating Investigative Beat 3, “The Credential.” During the post-mission 0200 conversation—Beat 4, “What Ducky Does With It”—Walker determines that the team finally has enough and authorizes the unauthorized confrontation.

“AEGIS Falls” is the compound finale rather than two competing end concepts. Its operational phase preserves the existing Russian undersea-threat asset against France; its final movement carries Ducky and AEGIS to the GenCorp-connected estate in Aix-en-Provence for the confrontation with Lieutenant General Callum Tremblay. Tremblay's active ambiguity is resolved rather than deferred into the next installment, and the protected exchange remains: “AEGIS falls regardless.” / “Then we build something better.”

GODKILLER
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The third campaign follows Trevor “Spook” Gartner and Victor Two Bravo after CIA Director of Operations Robert Macmillan declares the team rogue at East McFarlane Island. The public and institutional record treats Edward Goldberg's story as closed, but Spook begins finding inconsistencies that should not exist if the architect of Riptide Red is truly dead. Active shell companies, financial movement, old access records, protected witnesses, surveillance traces, and the continuing behavior of the network all point toward the same conclusion: Goldberg survived the first game's confrontation and has used the war to disappear behind an official death.

Godkiller is therefore less a conventional pursuit than an investigation into whether an individual can be meaningfully removed while the system that protected him remains intact. The campaign's title refers not only to hunting a man with a self-created myth of untouchability, but to destroying the institutional certainty that allows powerful conspirators to declare themselves beyond consequence.

The campaign follows three connected operational tracks. Spook owns the Katja Budanova and Goldberg pursuit lane; Tripper follows Hartwell, Vela 2, and the buried strategic architecture; Smoke sustains clandestine resistance networks and covert movement. The three tracks are unified by accountability: each operation preserves people, evidence, access, or institutional leverage needed to ensure that the architects of the war cannot simply outlast it.

A hidden cache on East McFarlane Island provides one bridge into the campaign. Players who discovered it in the first game possess a symbol and coordinates that unlock an otherwise concealed Game 2 location containing an unredacted Riptide Red report written by Matthew Mullen, Victor Two Bravo's former handler under the callsign Regent. The report reveals deeper FSB and CIA penetration, evidence that the conspiracy predates PANDORA by decades, and material implicating institutions far beyond the figures exposed at Montoya Farm.

The interrupted transmission in Frankfurt, the manually redacted Montoya file, and Macmillan's revealing slip during the East McFarlane stand-down order become evidence that Regent was attempting to expose the conspiracy and was removed before he could reach Spook directly. His decision to hide the report rather than publish it creates the campaign's central moral problem: the truth is so broad, destabilizing, and institutionally implicating that releasing it during a world war could cause damage on a scale that no surviving authority can control.

Spook also returns to the consequences of a humane choice made in the first game. Simenon Navarre and Karyna Budanova, now living as Simon-Pierre Aubert and Katja Reimann in Évora, were given new identities through the CIA. Their apparent protection fails when the GenCorp, Palisade Systems, and Bastion Infotech surveillance architecture identifies them again. Katja's insider knowledge makes her one of the most valuable living witnesses against the corporate layer of the conspiracy.

La Avispa becomes a structurally important partner in the Goldberg pursuit rather than detached sequel bait. Her underworld contacts and history connect the witness-protection, financial, maritime, and covert-movement lanes. Mutual suspicion between her and Spook gradually becomes professional trust without forcing their relationship into a fully resolved romantic outcome.

The source-ready campaign ladder is:

1. THE WASP'S NEST — Oaxaca; Spook approaches La Avispa openly and receives the first usable Goldberg lead.
2. DEAD LETTERS — Miramar, Cuba; Cristóbal Reyes identifies Goldberg's initial movement and the Meridian Shield / GenCorp financial connection.
3. GHOST SIGNAL — Tripper activates the Montoya Farm access route, authenticates Hartwell, and recovers a Vela 2 fragment.
4. SAFE HOUSE — Spook and La Avispa protect and relocate Katja while La Avispa incurs a future favor to secure extended Los Sietes protection.
5. HAVERFORD — Panama City; a dark-money clearing-house operation leads the hunt into the Caribbean continuity network.
6. REGENT — Mullen resurfaces, confirms the Senate Intelligence Committee route, and gives Spook a seventy-two-hour window before Crodyn's communication reaches Goldberg.
7. BEARER INSTRUMENT — Curaçao; Spook uncovers the shell-company, registration, and supply architecture concealing Goldberg's continuity vessel.
8. CROSS-CURRENT — Spook and La Avispa infiltrate a supply vessel, enter the NV Atlantis through its aft wet dock, and prevent the destruction of the KINGMAKER archive.
9. KING NOTHING — Spook defeats Bisutrí's final defense, reaches Goldberg's private decks, rejects Macmillan's second intervention, and captures Goldberg alive.

The former Game 2 reuse of “Come Fly With Me” and “Sins of Our Fathers,” together with the proposed return to the Found BFA-2 and East McFarlane Island, is residual duplication and is superseded. Those titles and locations remain exclusive to the Game 1 approach and climax. Goldberg's eventual capture does not occur on East McFarlane Island.

In “Bearer Instrument,” the investigation reaches Curaçao's financial and maritime-service infrastructure. Regent's intelligence establishes that Crodyn's expected communication will pass through a continuity contractor linked to several GenCorp-adjacent holdings. Spook and La Avispa obtain the vessel's true registration chain, access credentials, supply route, ownership structure, and evidence connecting it to Meridian Shield. El Tiburón's earlier favor also comes due, forcing them to preserve a compromised intermediary who can authenticate the shell-company transfers instead of treating the witness as disposable evidence.

The trail leads to the NV Atlantis, a purpose-built nuclear-powered oceanic residence, research, hospitality, and disaster-continuity vessel assembled through an international contractor group involving German, Israeli, American, and Russian companies. Publicly, Atlantis is a civilian floating city and resilience demonstrator. Secretly, KINGMAKER influenced its design from the beginning so that it could function as a mobile continuity state and hold the full KINGMAKER contingency archive.

Atlantis remains at sea for extended periods and receives supplies through tenders and service boats. It contains luxury residences, public venues, an advanced hospital, a jail, desalination and water-recycling systems, closed and filtered air sectors, a compact nuclear reactor with diesel auxiliary propulsion, a hardened mobile data center, a pneumatic delivery network, internal public transit, and a three-deck logistics-road system used by service vehicles. Several restricted private decks allow Goldberg to live with the comfort and territorial control of a hidden head of state while most passengers and ordinary crew remain unaware that he is aboard.

The ship's institutional invisibility is more important than literal concealment. Atlantis can be observed at sea, but its ownership, registration, manifests, crew records, restricted spaces, and operational purpose are fragmented across shell companies and state-linked contractors. Goldberg is absent from every public record. Authorities may know where the ship is while lacking admissible evidence that he or the KINGMAKER archive is aboard.

Atlantis appears civilian and unarmed to outside observers. Monitoring, safety, and crime-deterrence drones are visible above deck and can be justified as ordinary security measures. CIWS mounts, countermeasure launchers, armed aerial drones, autonomous anti-air, anti-surface, and anti-submersible craft, and other combat systems remain concealed in retractable housings and disguised service bays. Restricted decks contain heavier enforcement drones and automated security systems that cannot plausibly be shown in public passenger areas.

“Cross-Current” begins with Spook and La Avispa infiltrating a legitimate supply vessel already cleared to enter Atlantis. They remain covert until the tender reaches the aft wet dock. The early progression moves through cargo-processing areas, tender berths, inspection stations, maintenance spaces, the internal road network, public promenades, and restricted service architecture, gradually exposing the armed continuity vessel hidden behind the civilian city.

Bisutrí's contingency plan is to force Atlantis's nuclear engineers to initiate a controlled reactor shutdown. KINGMAKER deliberately linked that safety state to the physical lockdown of the mobile data center. Once shutdown begins, continuity bulkheads seal, data-center access routes lock, ventilation sectors isolate, and the archive enters an emergency state. Bisutrí then intends to evacuate Goldberg by airlift and detonate pre-positioned scuttling charges. Massive structural damage would trigger the data-center administrator AI's wipe protocol and destroy both active storage and protected physical media.

Spook and La Avispa reach the reactor-control sector, free or protect the engineers, and prevent completion of the controlled shutdown. The reactor remains operational, the data center stays accessible, and the scuttling sequence loses its full authorization path. Bisutrí escapes deeper into the vessel, leaving part of his squad behind to delay pursuit.

“King Nothing” shifts upward from industrial and engineering spaces into Goldberg's deserted private decks. Bisutrí and the remaining armed contractors make their final stand inside an indoor park-like recreation area containing mature plantings, artificial waterways, elevated paths, glass partitions, climate controls, maintenance access, and simulated daylight. The battle destroys the controlled paradise Goldberg built around himself.

After Bisutrí and his remaining detail are defeated, Spook reaches Goldberg's panic room. Goldberg unexpectedly unlocks it. He understands that the ship is compromised, his airlift has failed, his personal detail is gone, and the archive is being isolated from his control. Opening the room allows him to preserve the appearance that he is still choosing the terms of the encounter and to attempt to bargain his way out.

Goldberg argues that Spook lacks an institution capable of containing the archive and expects Macmillan to intervene. A few seconds later, Macmillan contacts Spook, repeats the East McFarlane stand-down pattern, and orders him to secure Goldberg in place and relinquish the vessel and recovered material to an incoming agency team. Spook demands full authentication. When Macmillan hardens his tone, he addresses Spook as “Gartner,” never by rank. Spook states that the Senate Intelligence Committee has notice of the operation, evidence, and recovery, then orders Macmillan to authenticate and place his name on the transfer. Macmillan goes silent and disconnects.

With Goldberg's last institutional escape route gone, the confrontation ends:

Goldberg: “You came all the way here to kill a dead man?”

Spook: “No. I came to bury you. Somewhere no one will find you until we're ready to bring you back from the dead.”

Spook restrains Goldberg, searches him, and places an opaque black transport hood over his head. La Avispa recognizes that Macmillan's announced incoming team makes her continued presence dangerous. She asks whether Bisutrí's airlift may still be waiting and deadpans that she hopes the pilot is still alive. Spook rejects the air route because it would be conspicuous and because he does not control Atlantis's air defenses.

Using Goldberg's biometric access, Spook takes the private elevator directly to the aft marina with Goldberg and La Avispa. They find two fueled executive speedboats prepared for the failed contingency evacuation. Spook secures Goldberg inside one while La Avispa retrieves the ignition keys from the marina office.

Spook contacts Regent's trusted network and arranges an open-sea pickup with no aircraft, transponder, or ordinary manifest. La Avispa takes the second boat. She explains that she cannot risk being seen even by Regent's people and that this is where she and Spook must truly part ways. She departs alone into the night. The exchange remains restrained and personal, with Spook acknowledging her as Yelena rather than reducing her to the La Avispa identity.

The boat departure is cinematic rather than playable. Spook leaves Atlantis with the hooded Goldberg secured behind him, then meets an unmarked service vessel before dawn. After challenge-and-response authentication, he transfers Goldberg to Regent's trusted associates under strict compartmentation. Goldberg remains publicly dead, but he is now alive in a protected evidentiary process where the people who previously extracted or silenced him cannot reach him.

The campaign closes later outside a secure communications room at an undisclosed location. Spook receives the message “Ask me again.” He asks for a moment before entering the room, then answers, “Okay. I'm ready.” The reinforced door closes behind him.

After a brief silence, Jimi Hendrix's “All Along the Watchtower” begins as the campaign play-out track. The GODKILLER end title appears over black and leads into the campaign completion sequence. The music choice remains subject to commercial licensing.

DOWNSTREAM CONTINUITY — DARK SKIES

Spook's Game 3 campaign is not an undeveloped destination. Dark Skies is source-landed as a four-act, thirteen-mission working campaign: Bad Omens, Private Route, Wrong Signature, and Dark Skies. Its opening block runs through “Dawn of Mercy,” “Mercy Line,” “Order and Progress,” and “Outer Port.” “Dawn of Mercy” is the Seattle relief-ship and civic-logistics opening. “Outer Port” is the Tomakomai, Hokkaido contact mission in which Spook meets Denis “Sakhalin” Malinin of Procella.

The campaign then shifts through the private-route phase into the older La Avispa / Laos-to-Yunnan material. The Yunnan crossing is retained as an interactive, low-input, high-tension sequence rather than a passive cinematic, and the later wrong-signature and strategic-architecture phase brings Lin Yueh-ting, also known as Y. T. Lin, from a theoretical objective into an active campaign figure. Wei Junlong, the Bastion–Atlas–GenCorp overlap, and Goldberg's wider-war articulation complete the expansion beyond the domestic-coup frame.

[DEVELOPMENT GAP — GODKILLER TO DARK SKIES TRANSFER] The Godkiller final operation and Goldberg consequence state are now developed. What remains open is the exact chronology between the Atlantis handoff and “Dawn of Mercy,” the post-campaign status of Spook, Tripper, Smoke, Regent, and La Avispa, the institutional consequences of Macmillan going dark, and the precise narrative mechanism that places Spook inside the already established Seattle–Brazil–Tomakomai–Yunnan Game 3 spine. Godkiller also still lacks a repository-verifiable stable timeline-track ID.
