TITLE LOCK — PATHFINDER
PATHFINDER is the canonical prologue title.

The title describes Romeo’s strategic function rather than a new unit designation, callsign, rank, or formal billet. He is being sent ahead of the conventional advance to find a viable human route through occupied territory, verify whether the suspected resistance can become a trustworthy partner, and help prepare the local intelligence, communications, logistics, access, and legitimacy that the later campaign will need.

The title also pays off at Cumberland Gap. Romeo’s work does not clear a literal route by itself; it helps create the people-and-network path that allows the AFC and conventional force to meet, preserve the corridor, and continue northeast. PATHFINDER should therefore read as restrained operational language, not chosen-one mythology.

CORE BRIDGE
After the Battle of Coronado, Romeo’s performance is recognized inside the surviving U.S. command structure. The recognition is based on the full record visible to senior planners: his conduct during the European fighting withdrawal, protection of the multinational AEGIS infrastructure chain, return to the occupied United States, urban fighting in Southern California, route-preservation work, and leadership during the defense of North Island.

The prologue must not turn this into a public victory ceremony or a chosen-one promotion. Romeo remains a staff sergeant. His reward is access, trust, and a more sensitive assignment. He is brought into a compartmented planning circle formed by surviving U.S. special-operations command personnel working with government intelligence and law-enforcement agencies on support to resistance inside occupied American territory.

The command cell presents recently decrypted Russian occupation communications and intercepted traffic analysis. The intelligence does not reveal a mature named organization. It indicates low-volume but persistent organized resistance activity in and around Calloway, Kentucky: unexplained supply losses, communication disruptions, local movement the occupation cannot fully account for, and Russian concern that separate incidents may reflect a coordinated human network.

The evidence is meaningful but incomplete. Command does not know who leads the activity, whether the people involved are reliable, how deeply Russian counterintelligence has penetrated the area, or whether there is a viable cell at all. Romeo is selected because his recent record demonstrates the specific combination the mission needs: small-unit combat leadership, coalition work, operating under degraded command, preserving routes and infrastructure, reading local conditions, and accepting responsibility without requiring a clean conventional battlespace.

TASKING
Romeo is ordered to enter occupied eastern Kentucky covertly and make contact with the suspected resistance activity around Calloway.

His initial mission is to:
- confirm whether an organized local resistance network actually exists;
- assess its competence, security, motives, and civilian legitimacy;
- help it survive immediate occupation pressure;
- develop communications, routes, intelligence handling, logistics, and operational discipline;
- connect credible local actors without automatically absorbing or subordinating them;
- create a durable resistance capability that can continue operating while the conventional campaign develops elsewhere.

Romeo is not sent to arrive as Calloway’s liberator, impose an outside command structure, or recruit a disposable proxy force. His brief gives him broad mission latitude because the assignment depends on local judgment and time. The command objective is to establish a trustworthy resistance partner and preserve future operational access, not to demand an immediate spectacular attack.

TIME-WISE CONVERGENCE
The two plans converge during the late American Freedom campaign.

By TRI-STATE POINT / the Battle of Cumberland Gap, the resistance Romeo helped develop has become the American Freedom Corps, and forward elements of the wider U.S. advance have reached the Cumberland region. The AFC’s local network, intelligence, route control, and civilian legitimacy combine with conventional mobility, engineers, aviation, and heavier logistics to preserve and liberate the corridor.

After Cumberland Gap, larger formations can exploit the opened route toward southwestern Virginia and farther northeast. This payoff must read as the completion of the preparatory strategy shown in PATHFINDER: Romeo was sent ahead to help create the human and operational conditions that a later conventional advance would need.

This does not mean Romeo personally commands the California-to-Virginia counteroffensive or that Game 2 depicts a continent-spanning campaign through his perspective. American Freedom remains concentrated in Kentucky and the adjoining Appalachian tri-state region. The wider advance is the parallel clock that eventually reaches the local story.

CHARACTER FUNCTION
The bridge marks a change in Romeo’s relationship to command.

In Game 1, he repeatedly keeps routes open for other people while operating inside inherited military structures. PATHFINDER shows senior command recognizing that ability and assigning him a mission where the structure is not yet present. His task is no longer only to lead soldiers through a route. It is to help people build the network, discipline, trust, and legitimacy that make a route possible.

The prologue therefore begins his soldier-to-resistance-organizer arc without completing it in advance. Romeo enters with military authority and operational skill, but he still has to learn that local legitimacy cannot be issued in a briefing room.
