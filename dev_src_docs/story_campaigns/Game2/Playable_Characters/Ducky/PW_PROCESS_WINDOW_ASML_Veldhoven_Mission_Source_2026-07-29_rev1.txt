3. MISSION IDENTITY
===============================================================================

Working title: PROCESS WINDOW.

The title is not yet owner-locked. It refers both to the tightly controlled
manufacturing tolerances required by advanced lithography and to the narrow period
in which AEGIS can prevent the sabotage operation from closing the West's recovery
path.

Location:
A fictionalized ASML campus and adjacent high-security logistics/integration complex
in Veldhoven, Netherlands.

The mission should use recognizable Dutch high-technology campus texture without
reproducing a sensitive real-world internal layout. The playable environment is an
authored abstraction built from office blocks, service roads, loading courts,
utility buildings, roof plant, secure transfer bays, landscaped separators, parking
structures, and controlled technical corridors.

Mission type:
High-value industrial defense, technical continuity protection, moving-asset escort,
and counter-sabotage operation with one scripted Lupin perspective transition.

4. TECHNICAL AND STRATEGIC FRAMING
===============================================================================

Do not reduce ASML or semiconductor manufacturing to one magical machine.

Within the wartime fiction, the protected target is the last immediately usable
Western high-end lithography integration and calibration path available to the DAOG
recovery effort. Its value comes from a combined system:
- a critical exposure-system module and associated precision-optics package;
- calibration, alignment, process-control, and qualification data;
- specialized tooling and contamination-control infrastructure;
- a small group of engineers and technicians capable of restoring and certifying
  the line;
- a supply and service chain already badly constrained by Taiwan's annexation and
  wider wartime disruption.

Destroying any one element would hurt. Destroying the combination would delay the
replacement of compromised AEGIS and autonomous-defense hardware long enough to
alter the strategic balance.

The enemy therefore does not need to occupy the entire campus. Its goal is to make
recovery nonviable by destroying the irreplaceable combination, contaminating the
technical environment, killing or abducting key personnel, and erasing the
qualification archive.

5. STORY SETUP
===============================================================================

Material recovered during SPLASHDOWN includes fragments of a wider target and
movement package. Halliday identifies repeated references to Dutch contractor
credentials, precision-optics shipping windows, and a protected transfer scheduled
at Veldhoven.

Dutch authorities are already dispersing staff and preparing to move the most
critical transportable components and data out of the threatened campus. AEGIS is
sent to reinforce the local operation, identify the real sabotage objective, and
keep the transfer corridor open.

The attackers are a deniable Chinese-directed technical-action cell rather than an
overt PLA assault force. They use compromised contractor access, locally sourced
vehicles, commercial drones, pre-positioned maintenance credentials, and a small
number of disciplined assault personnel. Some facilitators may be European
contractors or criminal intermediaries who do not know the full sponsor.

The mission should not conclusively expose the complete MSS chain or identify Wei
Junlong as the operational author. It should reinforce the pattern established by
ORBITAL DECAY: patient access, institutional camouflage, and an attack designed to
look like ordinary wartime infrastructure failure until it is too late.

7. PROVISIONAL MISSION FLOW
===============================================================================

The following flow is development scaffolding, not a locked mission outline.

PHASE 1 — CONTROLLED ARRIVAL

AEGIS enters a campus already under partial lockdown. Staff buses, contractor vans,
security barriers, abandoned bicycles, and emergency lighting show an evacuation
that began before the shooting. Dutch defenders are holding the public approaches,
but the attacker has used valid maintenance access to penetrate the service side.

The first objective is to reach the technical continuity team without firing through
sensitive production areas. Early contact is short and selective. The player learns
that the obvious machine hall is not the only target: the attackers are moving toward
an archive node, a transfer bay, and the roof-controlled route needed by the convoy.

PHASE 2 — SERVICE-SPINE DEFENSE

Ducky leads AEGIS through loading courts, utility corridors, plant rooms, and secure
crossovers to stop two sabotage teams from converging on the transfer package.

Gameplay emphasizes discrimination:
- hostile contractors and armed saboteurs are mixed with evacuating personnel;
- some systems must be shut down in a controlled order rather than simply destroyed;
- contamination doors, pressure zones, fire suppression, and power isolation can
  change routes without becoming puzzle-box spectacle;
- the player protects technicians long enough for them to complete a data and tooling
  transfer instead of treating them as an escort train that never contributes.

PHASE 3 — ROOF LINE / LUPIN TRANSITION

The protected convoy cannot leave because hostile overwatch has occupied several
flat roofs and elevated service structures covering the eastern transfer road.
Commercial drones are marking vehicles, a light anti-vehicle team is moving toward
the gate, and riflemen are using roof plant and maintenance housings to control the
open lanes.

Ducky tasks Lupin to lock down the approach. The transition rises from Ducky's
service-road position to Lupin on an adjacent utility/office roof. The player should
see the convoy, Ducky, and Dutch defenders below so that the overwatch function has
an immediate human and spatial relationship.

Lupin section register:
- weapon: his SCAR-H designated-marksman configuration;
- typical range: approximately 60–220 meters;
- pace: short, pressurized, and successive rather than slow precision sniping;
- targets: moving drone operators, a marksman pair, an anti-vehicle shooter, a breach
  pair using roof access, and a vehicle-team leader trying to close the gate;
- movement: rapid shifts between HVAC housings, parapets, solar structures, and a
  short maintenance catwalk rather than a static sniper nest;
- mechanics: shoulder switching, supported and unsupported aiming, rapid target
  prioritization, and protection of moving friendlies;
- tone: controlled aggression and immediate space-making, distinct from Frost's
  methodical long-range grammar.

The section ends when Lupin breaks the coordinated roof threat and reports the route
open. Control drops back to Ducky as the convoy begins moving. Lupin remains visible
or audible above, continuing to hold the rear while Ducky resumes the ground fight.

PHASE 4 — TRANSFER-BAY SABOTAGE

The roof threat was not the main demolition effort. A technical cell has reached the
transfer bay and initiated a layered destruction sequence designed to:
- corrupt or erase calibration and qualification data;
- damage the precision module during movement;
- trigger contamination and fire-control responses that make recovery impossible;
- trap the engineering team inside the secure zone.

Ducky and the local technical lead identify the correct order of intervention. The
player secures the bay while technicians isolate the dangerous systems. Combat and
technical action remain simultaneous but readable: AEGIS creates time and space;
the specialists save the asset.

PHASE 5 — CONVOY GATE

The surviving hostile element attempts a final vehicle block at the outer service
gate. The encounter is compact. Dutch defenders, AEGIS, and the convoy crew act as a
coordinated force. The player clears the immediate obstruction and protects the
vehicles through the gate rather than fighting a second full-scale battle.

PHASE 6 — PARTIAL VICTORY

The protected module, the qualification archive, and the essential engineering team
survive. The attackers nevertheless destroy secondary tooling, damage support
infrastructure, contaminate part of the technical environment, and disrupt the wider
service chain.

The result preserves a viable Western recovery path but does not restore production
or remove the semiconductor bottleneck. The mission buys time. It does not solve the
strategic problem.

Recovered attacker equipment and contractor-routing data point toward a mobile EW
and logistics architecture moving through Latvia. Halliday uses that lead to task
HARD WINTER. The later mission still owns Investigative Beat 2 and the discovery of
Nexus Meridian / KINGMAKER-linked architecture.

9. LUPIN CHARACTER AND GAMEPLAY LOCKS
===============================================================================

The broad Lupin perspective-transition asset is retained. Its exact implementation remains deferred.

It must:
- arise from a genuine moving-friendly threat that Ducky cannot solve from ground level;
- be shorter and more urgent than a Frost precision section;
- use closer ranges, moving targets, and rapid prioritization;
- visibly protect Ducky, Switchblade, Dutch defenders, engineers, and convoy vehicles;
- return control to Ducky immediately after the route is reopened;
- preserve Ducky as the mission's opening and closing playable perspective.

It must not:
- turn Lupin into a stationary long-range sniper;
- duplicate DARK FIBER's exact geometry or encounter rhythm;
- become a disconnected score-attack gallery;
- let Lupin single-handedly defeat the entire sabotage operation;
- use a loading screen or chapter break for the perspective transition.

10. ENVIRONMENT AND TONE — WORKING DIRECTION
===============================================================================

Use:
- low Dutch sky, damp wind, intermittent rain, and hard industrial light;
- modern but practical office/technical architecture;
- flat roofs, roof plant, maintenance stairs, service bridges, loading bays, security
  glazing, utility fencing, landscaped drainage, and controlled transfer routes;
- evacuation residue: badges, carts, cases, discarded protective clothing, parked
  bicycles, contractor signage, and interrupted ordinary work;
- restrained damage that becomes strategically serious because of precision and
  contamination rather than giant explosions.

Avoid:
- cyberpunk fabrication halls;
- a single glowing “chip machine” treated as the whole semiconductor industry;
- broad destruction of the real ASML campus;
- science-fiction holographic interfaces;
- a generic airport or warehouse raid;
- portraying Dutch partners as helpless;
- triumphal resolution of the global chip shortage.
