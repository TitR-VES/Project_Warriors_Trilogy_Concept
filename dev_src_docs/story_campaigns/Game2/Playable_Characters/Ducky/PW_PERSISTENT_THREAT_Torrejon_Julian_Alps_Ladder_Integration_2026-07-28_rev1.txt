MISSION 03 — ORBITAL DECAY

Torrejón Air Base / Torrejón de Ardoz, Spain.

Historical/descriptive label:
TORREJÓN DE ARDOZ OPERATION.

Core premise:
Ducky and AEGIS respond to a hostile penetration of the NATO/AEGIS satellite-command layer at Torrejón. The attacking force is composed of unmarked Chinese operators, superseding the older all-Russian attribution in legacy Game 1 material.

Operational objective:
- regain access to the satellite-command facility;
- interrupt a forced deorbit sequence targeting the AEGIS constellation;
- identify how the attackers penetrated a protected NATO site;
- preserve command, authentication, and telemetry evidence before sanitization.

Outcome lock:
AEGIS and local defenders interrupt the sequence before the full constellation can be lost. One satellite is nevertheless successfully deorbited. Its descent track leads toward the Julian Alps in Slovenia.

Narrative-title function:
ORBITAL DECAY names both the literal loss of orbital stability imposed on the satellite and the campaign's larger erosion of trust in the systems, credentials, and institutions that are supposed to keep the constellation secure.

Narrative function:

- demonstrates that compromised access and institutional penetration are as dangerous as direct assault;
- deepens the campaign's central problem: the official attribution picture is structurally incomplete;
- creates the immediate objective for the next mission.

MISSION 04 — SPLASHDOWN
Location:
Julian Alps, Slovenia.

Historical/descriptive label:
JULIAN ALPS RECOVERY.

Core premise:
The ORBITAL DECAY satellite impacts across a difficult Alpine debris field. Ducky and AEGIS deploy with Slovenian special-operations, civil-defense, resistance, and technical-recovery partners to secure the wreckage before hostile recovery teams can strip it for data or hardware.

Operational objective:
- locate the primary surviving satellite sections and security-critical components;
- establish control of the crash corridor and debris trail;
- deny hostile teams access to authentication, telemetry, storage, and AEGIS architectural material;
- recover enough evidence to reconstruct who initiated the deorbit and who intended to exploit the wreckage.

Outcome lock:
The allied force secures the decisive surviving material and denies hostile recovery. The recovery does not restore the lost satellite; it preserves evidence, limits compromise, and gives Halliday and DAOG a more dangerous but more accurate picture. Recovered material also warns of the Dutch high-technology target window that drives PROCESS WINDOW.

Narrative-title function:
SPLASHDOWN applies a controlled aerospace-recovery term to an uncontrolled mountain impact, creating deliberate irony while clearly identifying the mission as the physical consequence of ORBITAL DECAY.

Narrative function:
- pays off ORBITAL DECAY immediately rather than leaving the deorbit as an abstract briefing event;
- turns orbital systems warfare into a physical mountain recovery problem;
- reinforces that local Slovenian knowledge, authority, movement, and survival expertise are indispensable;
- broadens the attribution and compromise picture before PROCESS WINDOW without replacing HARD WINTER's authored investigative function.

Enemy-attribution caution:
Legacy text describes unmarked Russian recovery teams. The current controlling source does not automatically erase that possibility, but it also does not lock every hostile element at the crash site to Russia. Preserve the possibility of multiple interested hostile services or deniable teams until the dedicated mission source finalizes the recovery-force composition.
