II. RECOVERED TACTICAL / PROCEDURAL ARC
===============================================================================

The older campaign was built around a compact sequence:

COLD OPEN -> DEAD FREQUENCY -> HARD WINTER -> PERMAFROST -> HULL BREACH -> AEGIS FALLS

The current thirteen-mission ladder expands that structure but does not erase its
procedural logic. The surviving mechanism is:

1. battlefield failures expose a systems problem;
2. AEGIS interdicts an EW node and discovers internal parameters;
3. AEGIS tracks the mobile logistics and contractor architecture supporting the nodes;
4. physical relay/certification sites reveal the intended target and attack chain;
5. AEGIS seizes the live routing layer needed to reach or deny the active northern node;
6. the northern node is isolated before destruction;
7. its surviving authorization/credential trail points upstream to the intelligence
   and credential custodian layer;
8. the Volkov drive identifies the compromised senior authority;
9. Ducky moves outside the compromised institution to confront that authority.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FIELD-NODE AND CREDENTIAL MECHANISM
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Recovered technical rules:

- Hostile commands work because they carry valid authenticated override credentials.
  The affected systems are not simply “hacked” or confused; they accept the commands
  as legitimate high-authority instructions.
- The shadow credential system has a hierarchy.
- Field nodes possess intermediate certificates derived from a higher authority.
- Destroying an individual node destroys or denies its local key and capability but
  does not remove the root authority.
- Credential revocation cannot safely propagate through a communications architecture
  already controlled by the hostile credential chain.
- Effective denial therefore requires physical or out-of-band access to the node,
  route, or trust-anchor path.

This explains why PERSISTENT THREAT repeatedly requires physical assaults, exploitation,
line isolation, and controlled destruction rather than a remote software remedy.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- EW-node interdiction;
- two-element AEGIS/CORYS assault;
- exploit the technical floor;
- photograph the parameters;
- destroy the EW equipment;
- break contact under ARTEMIS artillery support.

The mission is therefore the first direct physical encounter with the hostile node
architecture, not merely an unrelated facility raid.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HARD WINTER
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Recovered tactical function:

The mission's procedural role is to move AEGIS from “who had the data?” to “how is the
system supplied, licensed, financed, moved, and certified?”

PERMAFROST
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Recovered tactical function:

- the Svalbard target is explicitly the third EW node;
- it is pre-positioned for a direct attack on the AEGIS datacenter;

SOURCE-BACKED RESOLUTION:
The synopsis can state the mission's target and procedural stakes more precisely.
AEGIS is not attacking a generic northern relay or an already active abstract strike
package. It is interdicting the third EW node before an authenticated authorization can
activate its direct attack on the AEGIS datacenter. The communications line must be
physically severed before the hardware is destroyed so the node cannot receive the
pending authorization, reroute, sanitize, or transmit a final authenticated action.
