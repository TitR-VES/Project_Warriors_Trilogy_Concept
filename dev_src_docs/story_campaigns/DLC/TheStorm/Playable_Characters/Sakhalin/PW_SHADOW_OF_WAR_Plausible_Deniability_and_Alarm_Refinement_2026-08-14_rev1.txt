01. PLAUSIBLE-DENIABILITY OBJECTIVE
================================================================================
The Russian command intent is not only to complete the mission kill against USS
Wyoming and withdraw undetected. The operation should, for as long as reasonably
possible after the team is gone, remain plausibly attributable to a catastrophic
technical or industrial accident rather than an obvious hostile sabotage action.

This gives the mission's low-casualty preference a concrete operational basis.
Bodies displaying obvious hostile-action injuries, missing personnel, gunfire,
or other unmistakable traces of an infiltrating team would cause U.S. personnel
and investigators to suspect deliberate sabotage much sooner and would weaken the
accident cover story.

Therefore:
- avoidance is the preferred solution;
- bypassing personnel is better than engaging them;
- unnecessary killing is operationally sloppy even when it does not immediately
  trigger an alarm;
- stealth kills remain permitted if required to prevent immediate compromise or
  preserve mission completion;
- the game must not reinterpret this rule as evidence that Malinin has already
  developed ideological sympathy for the Americans;
- his restraint here is professional discipline in service of the mission.

The game should not award a simplistic moral or "pacifist" score. Instead, the
consequence is systemic and narrative: casualties, discovered bodies, gunfire,
and obvious intrusion evidence make the cover story less credible and make the
mission harder to keep covert.

02. EARLY DIALOGUE — ACCIDENT COVER STORY
================================================================================
The team lead should state the casualty/deniability rule early in the mission,
preferably during the underwater approach after the three-hour-window exchange
or in the same introductory communications sequence.

English intent lock:
TEAM LEAD: Don't touch anyone unless you have to. When we're gone, this has to
look like an accident.
OPERATOR: And if someone gets in the way?
TEAM LEAD: Only if there's no other way.

Working Russian-language draft, subject to native-Russian and military-language
review before recording:
КОМАНДИР: Без необходимости никого не трогать. После нас всё должно выглядеть как авария.
ОПЕРАТОР: А если помешают?
КОМАНДИР: Только если иначе нельзя.

The intent matters more than literal word-for-word retention. Final Russian should
sound like experienced Russian special-operations personnel speaking naturally,
not an English tactical line mechanically translated into Russian.

The exchange should remain brief. No character explains the political concept of
plausible deniability to teammates who already understand why the instruction
matters.

03. RADIO-CARRIED ALARM THREAT
================================================================================
Most on-base U.S. personnel encountered in playable stealth spaces carry or have
immediate access to radios appropriate to their duties.

Gameplay rule:
- a person who positively detects the infiltrators will normally attempt to raise
  the alarm over radio quickly;
- the player receives only a short intervention window before the report is sent;
- if the player breaks line of sight before positive identification, the NPC may
  investigate rather than immediately transmit a confirmed intrusion report;
- once a confirmed base-wide alarm is successfully transmitted before charge
  initiation, the mission enters its existing effective-failure state;
- preventing a radio call does not automatically erase other evidence such as
  witnesses, discovered casualties, obvious gunfire, or an already-transmitted
  partial report.

The purpose is to make avoidance mechanically preferable. A player who repeatedly
approaches personnel closely enough to be seen is continuously gambling the whole
operation on stopping a transmission in time.

Most personnel should therefore be treated as potential alarm nodes, not passive
stealth-game obstacles whose only function is to be silently removed.

================================================================================
04. DETECTION / RESPONSE READABILITY
================================================================================
The player must be able to read the escalation state without turning the mission
into an arcade HUD exercise.

Useful presentation cues may include:
- an NPC reaching for or keying a radio;
- a short urgent voice transmission beginning;
- nearby personnel reacting to a suspicious or interrupted call;
- objective/status language indicating that a confirmed alarm is imminent;
- localized investigation after ambiguous sightings;
- wider coordinated response only after a report is successfully passed.

Exact UI treatment remains a production decision. The game should not rely on a
large universal countdown floating over every NPC.

================================================================================
05. CASUALTY DISCOVERY / COVER-STORY PRESSURE
================================================================================
If an American casualty is found before detonation, the response should reflect
that an apparent accident has not yet happened and a hostile-action explanation
has become much more credible.

At a design level:
- discovered bodies should sharply increase suspicion and local response;
- obvious gunfire or multiple casualties should make escalation faster;
- body-discovery pressure gives the player another reason to avoid unnecessary
  kills rather than treating hidden bodies as consequence-free collectibles;
- this need not become a body-hiding simulator unless later gameplay development
  explicitly chooses to support such mechanics.

A casualty that remains undiscovered during the playable mission can still weaken
the post-operation accident narrative. The player is not required to see every
later investigative consequence for the rule to matter in canon.

================================================================================
06. RELATION TO POST-DETONATION PHASE
================================================================================
Once the charges are initiated, preserving the immediate accident interpretation
is no longer a stealth-failure condition in the same way; alarms and emergency
activity are expected and the team's priority becomes escape.

However, the operation's earlier discipline still affects the narrative residue
left behind. A clean infiltration gives investigators fewer immediate indicators
of foreign sabotage than a path marked by casualties and obvious combat.

The established mission identity remains:
BEFORE DETONATION, YOU ARE AN INTRUDER.
AFTER DETONATION, YOU ARE A FUGITIVE.
