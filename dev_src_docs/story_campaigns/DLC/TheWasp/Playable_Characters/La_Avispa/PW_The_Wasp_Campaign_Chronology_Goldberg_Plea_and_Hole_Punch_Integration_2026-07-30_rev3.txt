A. Strategic background

PANDORA incapacitates or compromises a much larger share of United States military capability than Crodyn and the Riptide Red conspirators modeled. Its effects combine with the imminent outbreak of World War III in Europe, degraded American command-and-control, damaged domestic-response capacity, and the political confusion generated by the attack.

Russian, Cuban, Venezuelan, North Korean, and Iranian forces that had been complicit in the original arrangement enter or expand their presence under a diplomatic and purported peacekeeping rationale. The peacekeeping label is a ruse. It gives the deployments temporary deniability and political cover while the forces exploit the United States' sudden inability to coordinate an effective national defense.

The original bargain called for a bounded show of force or controlled intervention that would support Crodyn's emergency-power strategy and then withdraw. Once the depth of American vulnerability becomes visible, the foreign participants begin reneging on that bargain. They retain occupied territory, reinforce their formations, and use captured or controlled ports on the United States East Coast as sustainment corridors for additional personnel, equipment, ammunition, fuel, and supplies. Those ports allow the occupation to become self-reinforcing rather than temporary: follow-on forces can enter behind the first deployments, consolidate seized regions, and widen the gap between official diplomatic language and the military reality on the ground. What was designed as a managed conspiracy becomes an initially deniable but increasingly unmistakable land invasion of the mainland United States.

He allows himself to be arrested, negotiates a deliberately lenient agreement, and accepts a twenty-four-month federal sentence with eligibility for release after no less than six months, contingent on conduct, at Coleman Federal Correctional Facility.

The move serves several purposes at once:

- it places Goldberg inside a monitored federal institution where Crodyn cannot easily make him disappear without attracting attention;
- it publicly reduces Goldberg from suspected power broker to a white-collar defendant apparently contained by ordinary law;
- it limits the immediate usefulness of Crodyn's threat to expose him, because Goldberg has already entered a controlled legal process;
- it gives Goldberg time to preserve, compartment, or transfer surviving assets and contacts;
- it creates a fixed institutional environment that Los Sietes can study, penetrate, and eventually exploit;

The plea should not be portrayed as risk-free genius. Goldberg is gambling that Crodyn will respect the visibility of federal custody long enough for Los Sietes to build an extraction, that compromised officials will remain bought, and that the prosecution will not expand before he can disappear.
