Coronado Bridge approach
-> NASNI eastern gate / adjacent Coronado defense belt
-> base-defense command handoff
-> air-operations tower
-> southeast shoreline UAV-guidance fight

The route uses the installation's east-to-west depth, exposes the player to the

The KMZ should be treated as an objective skeleton, not a complete combat ladder.
Without additional enemy ground pressure between the marked nodes, the finale

landed flow below therefore locks combat functions between the mapped anchors

GEOGRAPHY CORRECTIONS AND NORMALIZATION

1. CORONADO BRIDGE

The bridge approach is a valid eastern entry route. It gives the finale a strong
arrival image and a clear transition from the southbound convoy into the defended
Coronado battlespace.

Use the bridge crossing as a vehicle-led opening under intermittent naval fire,
UAV threat, smoke, and damaged traffic control. Do not require a long unopposed
drive before the player reaches the base.

2. ADJACENT CIVILIAN AREA

The houses encountered before the NASNI gate should be described as evacuated
Coronado neighborhoods adjacent to the installation, not as a civilian population
evacuated “off North Island.” North Island is the naval installation at the north
end of the Coronado peninsula; the residential district is the City of Coronado
immediately east and southeast of the base.

The existing image remains valid: evacuated homes have been occupied, sandbagged,
or used as temporary positions by Navy and Marine defenders. The terminology is
what changes.

3. BASE-DEFENSE COMMAND HANDOFF

“Naval Facilities Command” is too institutionally specific and potentially
misleading as the first tactical headquarters. NAVFAC Southwest is headquartered
elsewhere, although Naval Base Coronado's Public Works Department has a NASNI
presence.

Use a fictionalized wartime label:

NASNI BASE-DEFENSE COMMAND POST

The mapped northeast administrative-sector position remains usable. The command
post can occupy a public-works or facilities-support building because utilities,
damage control, route clearance, and base defense have converged during the
attack. Its function is a fast in-motion handoff, resupply, and damage-control
picture—not a second full briefing scene.

4. TOWER COMMAND

The tower / air-operations position is geographically and functionally strong.
It should remain the primary tactical observation and tasking node. Tower
personnel identify the enemy UAV-guidance problem, explain which runway and
taxiway routes remain usable, and direct Delta toward the southeast shoreline.

The Base-Defense Command Post and Tower must have distinct functions:
- Base Defense: ground picture, damage control, access, reinforcement routes.
- Tower: air picture, UAV attack pattern, runway status, alert-aircraft timing.

5. ENEMY UAV-GUIDANCE ELEMENT

The southeast shoreline placement is credible as a short-range hostile observation
and guidance position operating from a low-profile boat or from a boat-supported
shore team. The element's destruction should reduce the accuracy and tempo of
enemy UAV attacks rather than switch all UAV pressure off.

The mapped “vantage point” should be the player observation / engagement point.
The hostile boat itself should remain visibly offshore rather than appearing to
sit on the land placemark.

A generic “nearby APC” is not sufficient implementation language. The supporting
vehicle must possess credible direct-fire capability against a small craft. Keep
the source generic as an ARMORED DIRECT-FIRE VEHICLE until the exact platform is
selected; likely presentation families include a Marine LAV/ACV-type cannon
vehicle or a CROWS-equipped vehicle. The player may designate the boat, direct
the vehicle, or briefly operate its weapon depending on the final gameplay pass.

6. NORTH ISLAND SMALL-ARMS RANGE

The owner marker labeled “North Island Gun Range” sits too far east for the
publicly documented range sector. The NASNI small-arms range belongs on the
southwestern Pacific-facing side of the installation, south of the Zuñiga sector,
with its surface-danger area extending offshore.

Move the mission handoff to a fictionalized SOUTHWEST RANGE-SECTOR STAGING POINT.
The range is useful because it naturally provides berms, cleared lanes, weapons
personnel, and a plausible Marine littoral fire-control team. It should not be
presented as a precise current-building reconstruction.

7. ZUÑIGA POINT

The owner marker for “Mark targets for NMESIS” is north of the actual southwestern
extremity. Zuñiga Point / the jetty lies farther south at the mouth of San Diego
Bay.

Move the final target-identification position to a fictionalized observation post
at or immediately landward of the Zuñiga Point sector. The player should not be
required to stand on the exposed jetty itself. A berm, damaged range structure,
temporary observation post, or shore-defense position can provide the needed
view toward hostile vessels south and southwest of the installation.

This correction strengthens the mission: the point is the natural place to see
the outer approach, the channel mouth, and ships maneuvering offshore.

8. NMESIS FIRING POSITION

The western coastal NMESIS position is plausible at planning scale. NMESIS does
not require the launch vehicle itself to have direct visual contact with the
target, but it does require a reliable target track, positive identification,
authorization, and deconfliction.

The player objective at Zuñiga should therefore be framed as:
- identify the correct hostile vessels;
- maintain the track through smoke, UAV interference, and maneuver;
- pass targeting data to the Marine fire-control element;
- confirm the launch window.

The strike should cripple or destroy one high-value vessel and force the remaining
ships to maneuver, break formation, or move farther offshore. It buys a window;
it does not end the entire amphibious battle by itself.

9. ENEMY IMPACT ZONES

These are naval-gunfire impact zones unless a separate land-artillery source is
later established.

The southwestern impact marker is on the North Island / Zuñiga side of the channel,
not at Naval Base Point Loma. Remove the phrase “Loma naval base structures” from
that marker. Use NORTH ISLAND SOUTHWEST SHORE / ZUÑIGA-SECTOR DAMAGE.

The west-central and northwestern airfield impact markers are useful visual anchors.
They should show destroyed support infrastructure and route denial while preserving
at least one usable runway and one launch path for the later alert-fighter sequence.

10. NCTS SAN DIEGO CLOSEOUT

NCTS San Diego is a valid NASNI tenant and a strong final destination, but it is
a communications command rather than the default senior air-station headquarters.

Use the location as an NCTS-HOSTED EMERGENCY OPERATIONS / COMMUNICATIONS CELL.
Primary command functions have been displaced or distributed because of shelling.
Delta reports mission success through the hardened communications node while the
base reconnects the ground, naval, and air picture.

This makes the final return narratively purposeful rather than a simple walk back
to another office.

LOCKED MISSION FLOW

PHASE 1 — BRIDGE UNDER FIRE

- Romeo, Fire Team Delta, and attached motorized U.S. elements cross the Coronado
  Bridge.
- Naval gunfire, UAV warnings, smoke, damaged traffic control, and defensive
  movement establish that the battle is already underway.
- The convoy enters the evacuated adjacent Coronado defense belt and reaches the
  NASNI gate.
- This phase must contain threat and spectacle; it cannot be an unopposed transit.

PHASE 2 — GROUND AND AIR PICTURE

- Delta receives a short in-motion handoff at the NASNI Base-Defense Command Post.
- The team moves across the active defense zone toward Tower Command.
- Combat or route-clearing pressure must occur between the two nodes so that the
  opening does not become two consecutive briefings.
- Tower identifies a hostile UAV-guidance element operating along the southeast
  shoreline and tasks Delta to remove it.

PHASE 3 — BREAK THE UAV GUIDANCE LOOP

- Delta advances through the hangar / service-road / perimeter corridor rather
  than walking exposed across open runways.
- The player reaches the mapped observation point and identifies the hostile boat
  or boat-supported guidance team.
- Romeo coordinates an armored direct-fire vehicle or uses a contextual weapon
  interaction to destroy the craft.
- Enemy UAV attacks become slower, less accurate, and less coordinated, but remain
  present as battlefield pressure.

PHASE 4 — REACH THE LITTORAL FIRE-CONTROL TEAM

- Delta moves west toward the corrected southwest range-sector staging point.
- A Marine littoral fire-control element confirms that an NMESIS battery can
  engage the shelling vessels, but only after positive identification and a
  stable target track.
- The range sector provides a short resupply, a handoff of targeting equipment,
  and a clear reason for Delta to continue toward Zuñiga.

PHASE 5 — ZUÑIGA POINT DEFENSE AND TARGET IDENTIFICATION

- Delta reaches a protected observation position immediately landward of Zuñiga
  Point.
- The player must identify the correct ships while rejecting friendly, damaged,
  obscured, or non-priority contacts.
- A ground counterattack, landing-party pressure, UAV harassment, or naval fires
  threaten the observation post during the designation sequence.
- The NMESIS launch occurs only after the player completes the identification and
  track-confirmation task.

PHASE 6 — TIDEFIRE STRIKE WINDOW

- NMESIS missiles strike the designated naval formation.
- One high-value vessel is destroyed or mission-killed; others break formation or
  pull farther offshore.
- Tower uses the temporary reduction in naval fire to launch already armed alert
  aircraft from the surviving runway / taxiway path.
- The fighters conduct a follow-up attack that compounds the disruption rather
  than replacing the ground battle.

PHASE 7 — RETURN THROUGH A CHANGED BATTLESPACE

- Delta returns northeast toward the NCTS-hosted emergency operations cell.
- The return must not be a clean retrace. It should use a vehicle pickup, a newly
  opened service route, or a compressed combat withdrawal while aircraft attack
  offshore and allied defenders push against the landing force.
- The player sees the consequence of earlier objectives: less accurate UAV attacks,
  reduced naval fire, alert aircraft airborne, and defenders regaining movement.
- Delta reports that the immediate naval assault has lost momentum.
- The campaign ends on a defensive success inside an unfinished war, preserving
  the Coronado-to-Calloway bridge into Game 2.

PACING AND LEVEL-DESIGN RULES

- Vehicle-led or compressed movement is required for the bridge, gate, and final
  return legs.
- Do not make the player cross active runways on foot without cover, route logic,
  and a specific combat purpose.
- Hangars, service roads, revetments, berms, maintenance structures, and perimeter
  routes should carry the infantry gameplay.
- The tower and NCTS sequences must not become redundant command-room scenes.
- The NMESIS strike is the operational climax; the alert-fighter launch is the
  visible payoff; the NCTS report is the emotional close.
- Preserve the sense that Romeo's small unit keeps routes and systems open for
  larger forces rather than winning the whole battle alone.
