================================================================================

1. BUILDING OCCUPANCY

The safehouse occupies the right-hand section of the building's second floor when the
facade is viewed from the street.

Production must preserve:

- a street-facing second-floor veranda or balcony zone;
- a readable relationship between interior rooms, shuttered openings, and the balcony;
- a view downward toward the team's parked black SUV;
- a believable route from the apartment to the street for the mission's departure;
- the sense that Victor Two Bravo is using a real residential apartment hidden inside
  ordinary Christiansted urban fabric, not a purpose-built agency bunker.

The KML coordinate controls map placement. The photographs guide facade character and
player-facing composition rather than centimeter-accurate geometry.

2. EXTERIOR ARCHITECTURAL LANGUAGE

The safehouse building should retain the historic Caribbean urban character visible in
the exterior references:

- pastel-blue or similarly pale, sun-faded plaster;
- a two-story mass with a covered upper gallery or veranda;
- thick vertical piers and repeated openings;
- ground-floor arches or deep shaded bays;
- louvered shutters and simple timber doors;
- shallow roof overhangs and ventilation openings below the roof line;
- salt, rain, heat, and age visible in the finish;
- minor cracks, patching, edge wear, stains, and uneven repairs;
- close adjacency to other historic buildings, parked cars, narrow sidewalks, and the
  ordinary visual clutter of a lived-in Christiansted street.

The building must not read as derelict or unsafe. It should read as old, imperfectly
maintained, and still fully inhabited.

LOCKED INTERIOR IDENTITY
================================================================================

3. CORE FEEL

The apartment is slightly antique, under-maintained, and strangely cozy.

The intended emotional contradiction is important:

- the place is old and imperfect rather than luxurious;
- paint, shutters, furniture, wiring, and fixtures show age;
- the team has filled it with operational material;
- despite this, the rooms feel sheltered, human, and briefly comfortable;
- it is a temporary covert workspace that still carries the residue of a home.

The environment should avoid both extremes:

- it must not become a polished boutique-hotel Caribbean interior;
- it must not become a filthy, horror-coded, abandoned safehouse.

4. ARCHITECTURAL AND MATERIAL CUES

The interior should draw selectively from the three interior references:

- high ceilings and tall wall planes;
- painted plaster in aged yellow, green, muted blue, cream, or related Caribbean tones;
- decorative ceiling borders or restrained historic trim;
- patterned encaustic-style, mosaic, or small-format tile flooring;
- tall timber shutters and louvered doors;
- arched or circular fanlights and transoms;
- occasional colored glazing capable of projecting restrained color onto the floor;
- simple dark timber furniture, mismatched chairs, old side tables, worn upholstery,
  and reused domestic storage;
- visible age in hinges, latches, slats, plaster edges, grout, and furniture finish;
- sparse wall art, old framed photographs, or local decorative remnants that predate
  Victor Two Bravo's occupation.

The geometric glazing visible in Reference 01 and the stained-glass fanlights visible
in Reference 02 are optional compatible cues, not a requirement to reproduce both in
every room.

5. LIGHTING LOCK

The primary living room is not lit by active electric room lighting.

Its illumination comes principally from strong exterior sunlight filtering through
closed or mostly closed shutters.

Production should preserve:

- narrow blades and broken bands of sunlight through shutter gaps;
- bright exterior luminance contrasted against a dim interior;
- dust, humidity, and suspended particles becoming visible in the light shafts;
- slow changes in light as exterior conditions and shutter movement vary;
- readable silhouettes of furniture, equipment, documents, and team members without
  flattening the room into uniform ambient fill;
- warmer bounced light near sunlit openings and cooler shadow deeper in the apartment;
- optional colored light from a fanlight or stained-glass section where used;
- no need for a horror-dark exposure level: the player must still navigate and inspect
  props comfortably.

The safehouse is introduced before the team departs for surveillance and later
infiltration. Its daylight state should contrast strongly with the after-dark
seaplane-base operation.
