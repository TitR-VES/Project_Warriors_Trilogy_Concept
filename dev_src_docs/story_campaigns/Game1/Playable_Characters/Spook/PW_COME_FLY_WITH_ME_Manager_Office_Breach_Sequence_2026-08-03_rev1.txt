Develop the manager's-office confrontation in COME FLY WITH ME as a two-state scene:

- VARIANT A — Victor Two Bravo reaches the office while the compound remains unaware;
- VARIANT B — the manager has locked himself inside after a compound alarm and the

This revision lands VARIANT A in rough source-ready form and preserves VARIANT B as

CORE ASSESSMENT

The proposed unaware-enemy version works. Its strongest qualities are:

- the team enters with a clear three-person role split;
- Spook remains the player's point of control and objective focus;
- Smoke and Tripper demonstrate synchronized competence without replacing the player;
- brief time dilation makes a very fast action legible without turning the scene into
  sustained arcade-style bullet time;
- the diegetic song creates darkly comic contrast while remaining part of the room;
- the manager interrogation immediately converts the breach into the aircraft-access
  objective;
- the scene remains compatible with non-lethal, suppressed-lethal, or mixed tactical
  resolution of the two office guards.

The principal implementation risk is synchronization. The music, door opening, team
movement, player input, guard reactions, and manager apprehension must align without
making the scene feel like a fixed cutscene. The solution is an adaptive semi-scripted
sequence with bounded movement, dynamic teammate target reassignment, and subtle
music-timing support rather than a fully locked animation.

VARIANT A — UNAWARE OFFICE OCCUPANTS
================================================================================

SCENE PRECONDITIONS

This branch is available when:

- the compound has not entered the full alarm state;
- no completed radio call or fixed-alarm activation has warned the office;
- the manager has not seen Victor Two Bravo on CCTV or otherwise received a warning
  that would cause him to lock the office;
- the manager's-office door remains unlocked;
- the manager and both office guards remain inside;
- Victor Two Bravo reaches the first-floor hallway without creating an immediate
  active firefight at the office threshold.

If any of these conditions fail, the mission transitions to VARIANT B or another
alert-state office approach rather than forcing the unaware choreography.

TEAM STACK AND ENTRY ORDER

Victor Two Bravo quietly stacks outside the manager's-office door.

1. SPOOK — first through the doorway.
   - Playable character.
   - Primary objective is to cross the threshold, dominate the room, reach the
     manager, prevent access to the manager's handgun, and secure him alive.

2. SMOKE — door operator and second entrant.
   - Positions beside the handle and opens or abruptly throws the unlocked door on
     Spook's signal.
   - Immediately follows Spook through the threshold.
   - Default responsibility is one armed office guard.

3. TRIPPER — third entrant and rear security.
   - Enters after Smoke.
   - Default responsibility is the second armed office guard.
   - Finishes the entry by covering the doorway and first-floor corridor through
     which the team entered, preventing a surviving guard or unexpected civilian
     from surprising the team from behind.

The exact left/right stack orientation remains dependent on the authored floor plan,
door hinge, furniture layout, and weapon-handling requirements. The role order is
locked even if the physical side assignments change.

PRE-BREACH AUDIO AND ROOM LIFE

Before the door opens, the player hears the office as a live occupied space:

- low conversation between the manager and the two guards;
- chair movement, paperwork, glassware, office equipment, or other restrained room
  texture;
- Frank Sinatra's "Come Fly With Me" playing from a radio or compact office sound
  system inside the room;
- wall and door occlusion muffling the track while Victor Two Bravo stacks outside;
- the song's opening becoming recognizable under the occupants' conversation as the
  team settles into position.

The song functions as diegetic world texture and ironic contrast, not as an external
combat score. It must remain spatially located inside the office. The track becomes
clearer and less occluded the instant the door opens.

MUSIC-CLEARANCE DEPENDENCY

"Come Fly With Me" is the preferred creative track and an unusually strong mission-
title payoff, but commercial music clearance remains a production dependency. The
scene must retain a fallback path using a newly composed or separately cleared
mid-century lounge / big-band recording with equivalent structural timing and tonal
contrast. No implementation should depend on copyrighted lyrics or an uncleared
master recording.

PLAYER PROMPT

Once the team is stacked and ready, the player receives a restrained functional
prompt:

    BREACH

The prompt should not over-narrate Spook's psychology. Its function is to let the
player initiate the coordinated entry while preserving first-person continuity.

The office door is unlocked in this branch. Smoke opens it abruptly rather than
using a forced mechanical breach. Exact animation may be a fast handle turn and hard
throw, a shoulder-assisted push, or another quiet-to-violent transition appropriate
to the door.

ADAPTIVE MUSIC SYNCHRONIZATION

The preferred authored timing is:

- the song's introduction is audible during the stack;
- the player is prompted as the arrangement approaches the bass-led transition;
- the door opens and the fastest entry movement begins as the bass line enters;
- the track continues under the room action and interrogation.

To preserve agency without audible looping or a visibly on-rails trigger, production
should use a narrow adaptive synchronization system:

- begin the office track when the team commits to the final stack;
- place the BREACH prompt inside a musically useful readiness window;
- allow very small, inaudible playback-rate correction or a hidden edit point in the
  radio source to align the door opening with the intended downbeat;
- never restart the song conspicuously if the player delays;
- if perfect synchronization cannot be preserved after an unusually long delay,
  prioritize player agency and diegetic continuity over exact beat matching.

The song should be mixed under weapon reports, impacts, dialogue, and barks rather
than overpowering them. Dynamic mixing may duck the radio slightly during essential
spoken lines while preserving the sense that the music is still physically playing
in the room.

SEMI-SCRIPTED FIRST-PERSON CONTROL

The breach remains first-person. The player retains partial control throughout.

LOCKED CONTROL PRINCIPLES

- The game does not cut to a third-person camera.
- The player initiates the breach.
- Spook receives bounded forward momentum across the threshold, but the player keeps
  meaningful look and aim control.
- The player can choose the immediate visual lane and can engage a threatening guard
  if necessary.
- The player cannot wander backward, leave the stack, or break the choreography during
  the short threshold-crossing beat.
- Once Spook reaches the manager's side of the office, normal first-person control
  resumes around the apprehension interaction.

The sequence should feel like the player is executing a rehearsed team maneuver,
not watching one.

TIME-DILATION PRESENTATION

The fastest part of the threshold entry uses brief, restrained time dilation from
Spook's point of view.

INTENT

- simulate the narrowed perception of an adrenaline surge;
- make the office geometry, two armed guards, manager, team lanes, and immediate
  threat decisions readable;
- preserve the impression of extremely fast coordinated violence while letting the
  player understand and negotiate it;
- avoid presenting the event as an ultra-fast blur or a fully authored cutscene.

DESIGN RULES

- Time dilation begins at or just after the door breaks the frame.
- It covers only the fastest threshold-crossing and initial threat-acquisition beat.
- It ramps quickly back to real time as Smoke and Tripper complete their takedowns and
  Spook reaches the manager.
- It must not become a reusable power, selectable ability, or prolonged "bullet time"
  state.
- Audio remains largely natural and intelligible. Any pitch or transient treatment
  should be subtle enough that the scene still feels diegetic rather than stylized
  beyond the project's grounded register.
- Production should tune duration by action readability rather than locking an exact
  time value at source stage.

ADAPTIVE OFFICE-GUARD CHOREOGRAPHY

Smoke and Tripper each enter with responsibility for one armed guard. Their takedowns
may be:

- unarmed control and incapacitation;
- suppressed lethal fire;
- one non-lethal and one suppressed-lethal action;
- another grounded combination supported by guard position, player aim, and alert
  behavior.

The variation exists to make the scene immediate and dynamic rather than to randomize
its outcome arbitrarily.

TARGET-REASSIGNMENT RULE

The choreography must react to player behavior:

- if Spook engages one office guard, the teammate assigned to that guard immediately
  shifts to the remaining threat, manager control, or doorway security;
- teammates must not fire redundant rounds into a guard the player has already
  neutralized;
- if a guard begins to aim at Spook or the manager's movement creates a collision,
  the nearest teammate may override the default lane to prevent an implausible delay;
- Tripper still ends the sequence covering the entry doorway unless immediate threat
  conditions require a temporary deviation;
- the team must not steal the manager apprehension from Spook.

This adaptive assignment is essential to maintaining player agency inside the
semi-scripted structure.

MANAGER APPREHENSION

Spook moves directly toward the manager while Smoke and Tripper deal with the two
armed guards.

The manager has access to a handgun but fails to draw it before Spook reaches him.
Exact carry or storage position remains open for the floor-plan pass. The key dramatic
facts are:

- the manager recognizes the breach too late;
- he begins to reach or orient toward the handgun;
- Spook controls him before he can present it;
- Spook strips and discards or retains the weapon according to the final interaction
  animation;
- the manager is captured alive and visibly terrified.

The scene should not require the player to shoot the manager. His live cooperation is
needed to obtain immediate aircraft access and his restrained end state remains
controlling.

DIALOGUE LOCK

Spook grabs and controls the manager, then says:

    SPOOK
    I need a plane. One that I can use. Now!

The terrified manager answers that he keeps one fueled for emergencies at the north
jetty. Exact wording may be adjusted for natural performance, but must preserve:

- the aircraft is already fueled;
- it is usable immediately;
- it is berthed at the north jetty;
- the manager understands that Spook wants practical access, not information about
  the company fleet in general.

Spook pauses only long enough to process the answer, then commands:

    SPOOK
    Keys!

The manager produces or indicates the Found FBA-2C keys. Spook takes them.

The brief pause before "Keys!" is important. It lets the manager's answer land,
keeps Spook controlled rather than breathless, and turns the command into the clean
objective-resolution beat.

RESTRAINT AND HANDOFF

After taking the keys:

- Spook motions to Tripper to secure the manager;
- Tripper zip-cuffs the manager to the office chair;
- the manager remains alive, disarmed, and restrained;
- Smoke confirms the room and immediate route state;
- Tripper resumes doorway / corridor awareness after completing the restraint;
- the team transitions toward the north jetty and the fueled Found FBA-2C.

If one or both office guards were subdued rather than killed, their restraint and
weapon-security state must be resolved before the team leaves. The sequence must not
silently allow an incapacitated but unsecured armed guard to recover immediately
behind Victor Two Bravo.

DARK-HUMOR AND TONE RULE

The diegetic song may give the scene a mildly surreal, darkly comic crime-thriller
contrast. The effect works only through restraint:

- no character acknowledges the song;
- no choreography becomes dance-like;
- no impacts are cut into a music-video montage;
- no repeated lyric-to-action joke is required;
- the room remains dangerous, abrupt, and professionally violent;
- the manager's fear and the team's objective focus remain real.

The music should make the sequence feel strange and memorable without making it feel
self-consciously overproduced.

FAILURE, RECOVERY, AND STATE TRANSITION

The unaware sequence cannot be allowed to softlock the mission.

- If the player creates a full alert in the hallway before initiating the breach,
  transition out of VARIANT A.
- If an office guard completes a radio warning before the door opens, transition to
  an alerted office state.
- If the manager receives warning and locks the door, use VARIANT B.
- If the player engages through the closed door or window before the stack completes,
  cancel the unaware choreography and enter a freeform alert response.
- If a restrained or incapacitated office guard later becomes a threat, the source
  must define persistence or recovery rules rather than relying on invisible cleanup.
- The manager, keys, and aircraft-access objective require fail-safes if animation,
  navigation, or AI interruptions occur.
