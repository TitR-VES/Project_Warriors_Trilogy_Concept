BREACH REACTION AND APPREHENSION
================================================================================

INITIAL FREEZE

When Smoke abruptly opens the door and Victor Two Bravo enters:

- the manager and both armed guards freeze for approximately half a second while
  processing the sudden entry;
- this reaction overlaps the restrained threshold time-dilation presentation;
- the freeze must feel like startled human recognition, not AI inactivity;
- all three begin different responses as the entry resolves: the guards react to
  Smoke, Tripper, and the player's lane while the manager commits to the handgun.

The exact duration remains subject to production tuning, but the intended read is a
very brief common shock followed immediately by divergent actions.

MANAGER WEAPON-REACH VARIANTS

The manager's response adapts to his current starting position.

SEATED RESPONSE

- The seated manager turns and reaches across the desktop for the handgun.
- His movement is urgent, clumsy, and too late rather than tactically proficient.
- Spook crosses the remaining distance before the manager establishes a grip.

STANDING RESPONSE

- The standing manager twists toward the desktop and reaches behind and to his side
  for the handgun.
- His prior leaning posture briefly compromises his balance and delays the reach.
- Spook closes before the manager can take possession of the weapon.

In both states, the manager must visibly decide to reach for the handgun. Spook is not
assaulting a compliant man without cause; he is interrupting an immediate failed
attempt to arm himself.

HAND CONTROL AND DESK IMPACT

Spook intercepts the manager's outstretched reaching hand and forcefully slams it
against the desktop before the manager can grasp the handgun.

The beat should present:

- Spook controlling the wrist or hand from first person;
- the manager's hand striking the desktop near the handgun, ashtray, and glassware;
- a sharp impact sound, small object movement, and plausible glass or liquid response
  without turning the desk into exaggerated breakaway scenery;
- the manager grunting in pain and reflexively withdrawing the arm;
- the handgun remaining available for Spook to seize immediately.

The impact is a rapid control action, not an extended punishment animation. It exists
to stop the weapon reach and create the opening for the disarm.

DISARM AND POINT-BLANK CONTROL

Spook picks up the manager's handgun from the desk and immediately uses it to control
him.

- Spook holds the front of the manager's shirt with his free hand.
- He drives or holds the manager into a controlled position against or beside the
  desk according to the active starting state.
- He places the handgun's muzzle point-blank beneath the manager's chin.
- The camera remains first-person and resolves into a close view of the manager's
  terrified face from Spook's perspective.
- Spook remains steady and professionally focused rather than visibly enraged.
- The manager is not fired upon in the successful authored branch.

The framing should be intimate and threatening without cutting to third person. The
manager's fear, breath, eyes, cigar smoke, and loss of composure carry the dramatic
weight.

OPENING INTERROGATION DIALOGUE
================================================================================

BREATH AND FIRST QUESTION

Once controlled, the manager involuntarily hyperventilates once or twice. After a
short beat, he stammers:

    MANAGER
    What do you want?

Spook does not answer immediately. He holds the manager in silence for approximately
half a second.

The manager attempts to fill the silence:

    MANAGER
    Look, if you're here for the money, take as much as you want. I don't care.

Spook allows another brief pause before answering. The pauses should feel like
Spook's controlled assessment and the manager's panic, not like dropped dialogue or
an unresponsive player prompt.

The exact connective wording may be performance-tuned, but it must preserve:

- one immediately usable aircraft;
- already fueled status;

Spook pauses briefly, then commands:

    Keys!

than reaching over the desktop from the visitor side.

- Normal first-person locomotion resumes for the short movement.
- The route around the desk must remain clear in both manager starting-state variants.
- Tripper and the restrained manager must not obstruct the player's path.
- The movement should take only a few seconds and should feel like completion of a
  practical objective, not an extended search puzzle.
- The handgun taken from the manager remains under Spook's control throughout the

DRAWER INTERACTION

When the player reaches the chair side and faces the indicated top-right drawer, a
restrained functional prompt appears:

    OPEN DRAWER

Activating the prompt plays a short first-person interaction in which Spook opens the
top-right desk drawer. The prompt should attach to the physical drawer, not float at
the manager's earlier position or activate through the desk.

KEY INTERACTION

The Found FBA-2C keys are visible inside the open drawer. A second, separate prompt
appears:

    TAKE KEYS

Activating the prompt causes Spook to pick up the floatplane keys and secure them on
his person. The objective updates only after this second interaction completes.
Opening the drawer alone does not count as acquiring aircraft access.

The two-step interaction is locked because it:

- preserves believable physical reach and desk geometry;
- gives the player a clear first-person objective action after the apprehension;
- prevents an automatic key transfer that would weaken player participation;
- separates discovering the keys from actually taking possession of them;
- provides a clean checkpoint and fail-safe boundary for the aircraft-access state.

INTERACTION FAIL-SAFES

- The drawer must remain interactable after minor physics disturbance to desk props.
- The keys cannot be knocked out of the drawer by unrelated gunfire or prop collision
  without a recoverable alternate pickup state.
- If the player looks away after opening the drawer, the keys remain present and the
  TAKE KEYS prompt returns when appropriately targeted.
- Tripper's restraint animation cannot push the manager into the player's interaction
  space or block the drawer.
- If accessibility settings reduce precision targeting requirements, the interaction
  volume may expand without allowing activation from the wrong side of the desk.
- A checkpoint should not save a state in which the drawer is open but the keys have
  become unavailable.
