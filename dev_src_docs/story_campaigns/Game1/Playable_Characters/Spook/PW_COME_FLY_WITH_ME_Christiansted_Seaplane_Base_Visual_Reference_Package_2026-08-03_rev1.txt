LOCKED VISUAL-DIRECTION FINDINGS
================================================================================

1. COMPACT WATERFRONT FACILITY

The base should read as a compact working harbor aviation facility rather than a large
conventional airport.

The visual language should emphasize:

- immediate contact between operations buildings, paved service areas, docks, and
  navigable harbor water;
- short walking distances and strong visual overlap between landside and waterside
  activity;
- low-rise construction rather than terminal-scale architecture;
- a practical working-site character in which aircraft servicing, passenger handling,
  utility equipment, and security coexist in a compressed footprint;
- open exposure to water, wind, salt, and tropical weather.

The map may enlarge or reshape this footprint for gameplay, but the environment must
not lose the compact harbor-base identity visible across the reference set.

2. TOPOGRAPHY AND TOWNSCAPE

The waterfront is visually backed by steep or strongly rising green terrain.

Production should preserve:

- dense tropical vegetation on the hillside;
- irregular low-rise development climbing or sitting against the slope;
- a layered background in which harbor, waterfront structures, palms, town fabric,
  and hillside are visible within the same broad composition;
- bright or warm roof colors and Caribbean waterfront color accents without turning
  the base itself into a resort environment;
- a sense that the facility is embedded in Christiansted rather than isolated on a
  remote military-style airfield.

The hill and town are important distant-or-midground anchors for player orientation,
especially when moving between the western approach, administration building, fuel
area, and north jetty.

3. PRIMARY OPERATIONS BUILDING LANGUAGE

The administration or operations building should derive its general material language
from the supplied photographic reference while remaining fictionalized for Ocean Blue
Sky Tours, Ltd.

Useful cues include:

- pale gray or off-white corrugated metal siding;
- a shallow or low-profile roof line;
- simple dark-framed rectangular windows;
- functional exterior doors without decorative treatment;
- exterior metal stairs and landings;
- wall-mounted air-conditioning units and service conduits;
- chain-link perimeter elements and practical gates;
- sparse low-maintenance planting rather than elaborate landscaping;
- weathering appropriate to salt air, humidity, sun, and rain;
- patched, stained, or repaired hardscape at the water-facing edge.

The final building must support the locked first-floor manager's office, north service
entrance, corridor, office-guard positions, alarm button, CCTV logic, and both manager-
office breach variants. The photographs guide visual treatment, not the exact interior
floor plan.

4. PAVED APRON AND SERVICE YARD

The paved areas should look used, exposed, and repeatedly repaired rather than newly
constructed.

Production cues include:

- faded concrete or light asphalt;
- expansion joints, patchwork, staining, tire marks, water marks, and drainage traces;
- small utility islands, barriers, bollards, cones, boxes, carts, tanks, and service
  vehicles placed with operational logic;
- broad open sightlines broken by equipment rather than dense architectural cover;
- clear aircraft movement and service zones near the water;
- enough clutter to feel real without compromising stealth readability or companion
  navigation.

The wide simulator overview is useful for understanding the broad apron-to-water
relationship, but it must not be copied as exact geometry.

5. WATERFRONT EDGE AND DOCK CONSTRUCTION

The references support a mix of hard shoreline, floating dock, gangway, and low utility
infrastructure.

The production environment should use a coherent selection of:

- low concrete seawalls or quay edges;
- floating dock modules with visible flotation and edge wear;
- metal or painted gangways and boarding bridges;
- mooring cleats, bollards, ropes, cables, hoses, and service lines;
- dock-mounted utility boxes or service cabinets;
- ladders, edge rails, rub strips, bumpers, and fenders where operationally credible;
- wet, salt-stained, algae-marked, rust-marked, and sun-faded surfaces;
- small vertical changes between fixed quay and floating dock that communicate tide
  and water movement.

Dock dressing must support player traversal and stealth readability. Decorative ropes,
hoses, and service cables may not become invisible collision traps.

8. WATER, WEATHER, AND TROPICAL ATMOSPHERE

The reference set supports:

- blue-green harbor water with small wind-driven surface movement;
- visible reflections and color variation near the quay and dock;
- bright tropical daylight in some views;
- fast-changing or broken-cloud Caribbean skies in others;
- palms and vegetation moving visibly in coastal wind;
- salt haze, humidity, sun bleaching, and localized staining.

COME FLY WITH ME's infiltration is after dark. Production should translate these
daytime references into a credible night version rather than reproducing daytime
lighting.

Night adaptation should include:

- dark water carrying broken reflections from dock and building lights;
- practical exterior security lighting rather than uniform cinematic illumination;
- localized pools of warm or neutral utility light;
- deeper shadow beneath wings, stair landings, dock edges, and palms;
- selective hillside and town lights preserving Christiansted's surrounding presence;
- readable silhouettes of the operations building, fuel tanks, jetty, aircraft, and
  shoreline;
- optional cloud movement and intermittent moonlight only where compatible with the
  mission's stealth requirements.

9. COLOR AND MATERIAL PALETTE

The base should balance a restrained industrial palette with Caribbean surroundings.

Recommended environmental palette direction:

- pale gray, off-white, faded blue, and weathered metal for the working facility;
- concrete gray, salt white, rust brown, algae green, and oil-dark staining at ground
  and water edges;
- blue-green water and saturated sky as the largest natural color fields;
- dense vegetation greens and palm tones;
- selective coral, red, pink, cream, or pastel accents in surrounding waterfront
  buildings and roofs;
- Ocean Blue Sky Tours branding as a fictional layer that does not copy real operator
  signage.

10. ENVIRONMENTAL STORYTELLING

The reference set supports a site that operates publicly as a legitimate aerial-tour
business while concealing Goldberg-linked transport activity.

Environmental storytelling may include:

- public-facing tour signage and modest passenger-handling elements;
- maintenance and service equipment consistent with daily floatplane operations;
- practical employee spaces and weathered operational notices;
- secure doors, controlled areas, radios, CCTV, and alarm infrastructure integrated
  into an otherwise ordinary commercial harbor facility;
- discrepancies between the cheerful Ocean Blue Sky Tours public image and the harder
  armed-contractor presence after hours;
- no implication that every civilian employee or harbor worker is complicit.

The references support authenticity of place. They do not authorize copying real
company marks, staff identities, or exact security arrangements.
