CORE ASSESSMENT

The proposed alerted version works because it is not merely Variant A with a stronger
door. It changes every major dramatic and gameplay layer:

- the office guards have left the room and become part of the active base pursuit;
- the manager has warning, a locked door, a firearm, cover, and time to call police;
- the player may worsen the danger by shooting through the door before the team is
  ready;
- entry requires a visible three-person breach procedure;
- the stun grenade changes the manager's physical and vocal state;
- Spook controls the manager on the floor rather than against the desk;
- the keys are carried in the manager's jacket rather than stored in the desk;
- Smoke receives the keys immediately inside the office;
- the manager is restrained to the desk rather than to the office chair;
- the completed or interrupted police call materially changes the escape pressure.

The scene should feel louder, rougher, and more urgent than Variant A while remaining
first-person, readable, and tactically coherent.

VARIANT B PRECONDITIONS AND STATE TRANSITION
================================================================================

CANONICAL VARIANT B PRECONDITIONS

The full authored Variant B sequence becomes available when:

- the base has entered STATE 2 — COMPOUND ALARM;
- the manager knows Victor Two Bravo is on the base;
- both armed office guards leave the office to pursue Spook, Smoke, and Tripper;
- the manager remains alive and reaches the office door before the team;
- the office door is still sufficiently intact to be locked and mechanically breached;
- the manager has access to his handgun and phone;
- the player has not already entered the office through another opening.

If only one office guard leaves, one remains trapped inside, or the door is destroyed
before lockdown completes, the encounter transitions to a hybrid or freeform alert
state rather than forcing this exact choreography.

OFFICE-GUARD DEPLOYMENT

When full alert is established, both office guards abandon their unaware seated
positions and move out through the office door to join the search for Victor Two
Bravo.

Their departure must be coordinated with the manager's movement so that:

- the manager does not lock either guard inside accidentally;
- the guards do not remain scripted at the office threshold while the team approaches;
- their radios and weapons remain active parts of the compound search;
- their survival or defeat outside the office remains governed by the broader alert
  encounter rather than by the manager-room sequence;
- the office is occupied only by the manager when the locked-door breach begins.

MANAGER LOCKDOWN

After the guards clear the doorway, the manager:

- closes and locks the wooden office door;
- takes possession of the handgun that normally rests on his desk;
- moves behind the desk and uses it as cover from the doorway;
- watches and listens for movement in the hallway;
- remains frightened but is actively prepared to fire if he believes the team is
  attacking through the door;
- begins an approximately ninety-second countdown to contacting police.

The manager is not presented as tactically expert. His defensive position is the
panicked action of a cornered civilian accomplice who has warning, a firearm, and a
familiar room.

================================================================================
NINETY-SECOND POLICE CALL
================================================================================

TIMER START

The approximately ninety-second timer begins when the manager secures the office door.
It is an authored baseline rather than an invisible arbitrary delay.

The delay represents the manager:

- processing the full alarm;
- listening to radio traffic and gunfire;
- taking cover;
- deciding that the threat cannot be handled by the private security detail;
- reaching and using his phone.

CALL ATTEMPT

At approximately ninety seconds, the manager uses his phone to call police.

The call must be a real encounter-state action rather than a purely decorative line:

- if the call completes, the external police response begins;
- the armed U.S. Coast Guard patrol-boat approach remains linked to the same broader
  escalation stage under the controlling security source;
- if the team breaches and interrupts the call before completion, the external
  response is not falsely marked as completed;
- if the manager is under immediate fire when the call window arrives, he may delay
  the attempt briefly and retry when able rather than calmly exposing himself;
- the phone state and completed-call state must survive checkpoints correctly.

DIALOGUE AND CALL-STATE RULE

The manager later says that the police are on their way as part of his locked alerted
dialogue. If the call has completed, the statement is factual. If the team breaches
before completion, the same frightened line functions as a bluff intended to make
Victor Two Bravo leave.

The actual external-response state must be determined by the completed call, not by
the dialogue line alone.

================================================================================
LOCKED WOODEN DOOR AND PLAYER APPROACH
================================================================================

DOOR PROPERTIES

The manager's office uses a destructible wooden door with a conventional locking
mechanism suitable for the authored shotgun breach.

The door must support:

- visible bullet impacts and splintering;
- partial damage without immediate total collapse;
- destruction of the locking mechanism during the coordinated breach;
- a narrow post-breach opening suitable for the stun-grenade throw;
- a fully broken state if the player applies excessive force before the stack forms.

PLAYER FIRE THROUGH THE DOOR

The player may fire at the locked door before the team completes the breach stack.
Doing so is permitted but dangerous.

If the player fires into or through the door:

- the manager may return fire from behind the desk through the damaged wooden door;
- his response is based on sound, impact location, and visible damage rather than
  perfect knowledge of Spook's exact position;
- incoming rounds may endanger Spook, Smoke, or Tripper during their approach;
- the manager may shift behind the desk between short return-fire attempts;
- the team calls out the increased danger and avoids standing exposed directly in
  front of the door.

The scene is notably safer if the player approaches quietly, avoids announcing the
team's exact position, and lets the stack form before initiating the breach.

PREMATURE DOOR DESTRUCTION

If the player destroys or opens the door completely before the coordinated breach:

- the authored Tripper / Smoke breach sequence is cancelled;
- the encounter becomes a freeform armed entry against the alerted manager;
- the manager, keys, police-call state, survival requirement, and weapon-security
  requirements remain active;
- the mission must not softlock because the BREACH prompt can no longer appear.

================================================================================
TEAM COORDINATION AND STACK
================================================================================

APPROACH ROLES

As Victor Two Bravo reaches the locked office:

- Spook remains the playable lead and first entrant;
- Tripper prepares the mechanical breach;
- Smoke prepares the stun grenade and supports doorway control.

TRIPPER'S BREACHING WEAPON

Tripper retrieves a Remington 870 / Serbu Super-Shorty breaching shotgun from a
quiver-like back holster or scabbard.

The weapon is configured for the authored breach with:

- breaching rounds;
- a breaching muzzle adaptor;
- a compact presentation suitable for carriage as a dedicated tool rather than his
  primary fighting weapon.

Tripper takes position at the locking side of the door and points the breaching
weapon at the required downward angle toward the locking mechanism.

The animation must communicate that Tripper is addressing the lock and directing the
shot safely into the door structure rather than aiming into the occupied room.

SMOKE'S STUN-GRENADE PREPARATION

Smoke readies a stun grenade while maintaining enough control to avoid exposing it or
prematurely initiating the throw.

He positions so that he can:

- reach the narrow opening created by Tripper;
- throw the grenade promptly after the lock is defeated;
- avoid blocking Spook's entry lane;
- transition into the room immediately after Spook and Tripper.

SPOOK'S POSITION

Spook stacks as the first entrant and remains responsible for closing on the manager,
disarming him, and securing him alive.

READY STATE AND PLAYER PROMPT

The BREACH prompt appears only when:

- Tripper has the shotgun in position;
- Smoke has the stun grenade ready;
- Spook is aligned for first entry;
- companions are not obstructed or under immediate unresolved fire;
- the door remains in a valid breachable state.

The player then receives:

    BREACH

The player initiates the sequence. The team does not breach automatically merely
because all three characters have reached the door.

================================================================================
MECHANICAL BREACH AND STUN-GRENADE ENTRY
================================================================================

LOCK DEFEAT

After the player initiates BREACH:

- Tripper fires two quick breaching rounds into the locking mechanism;
- the lock and surrounding wood disintegrate or fail visibly;
- the door cracks open by several inches rather than flying fully inward;
- splintering, smoke, and debris remain readable without obscuring the grenade gap;
- the manager may react vocally or shift behind the desk, but the coordinated action
  proceeds too quickly for an effective aimed response through the new gap.

STUN-GRENADE THROW

Smoke immediately:

- pulls the stun-grenade pin;
- controls the lever until the throw;
- lobs the grenade through the narrow opening into the office;
- clears his throwing hand and prepares to enter.

The throw must be robust against door-angle variation and cannot allow the grenade to
bounce back into the hallway because of minor animation or physics error.

PROTECTIVE CAMERA LOOK

As the grenade enters the room, Spook's first-person view is pulled briefly away from
the doorway gap to avoid direct exposure to the detonation.

The movement should be:

- short;
- physically motivated;
- less severe than a hard cinematic camera takeover;
- compatible with motion-reduction and flash-sensitivity accessibility settings;
- immediately releasable if an unexpected hallway threat requires player control.

DETONATION TIMING

Approximately two seconds after the throw, the stun grenade detonates inside the
office.

The blast produces:

- an intense but brief interior flash;
- concussive sound and room response;
- disorientation of the manager;
- disturbed desk objects, dust, and loose paper where appropriate;
- no arbitrary lethal injury to the manager in the successful authored branch.

ENTRY ORDER

Immediately after detonation, the alerted entry order is:

1. SPOOK — first entrant and manager-control lead.
2. TRIPPER — second entrant, following from the lock-breach position.
3. SMOKE — third entrant after completing the grenade throw.

This order differs intentionally from Variant A. Tripper is already positioned at
the threshold after defeating the lock, while Smoke completes the grenade action
before following.

The three enter in rapid succession. The scene remains first-person and does not cut
to an external cinematic camera.

================================================================================
MANAGER DISARM AND FLOOR CONTROL
================================================================================

MANAGER POST-DETONATION STATE

The manager remains behind or near the desk but is heavily disoriented by the stun
grenade.

He may:

- struggle to orient toward the doorway;
- retain an incomplete grip on the handgun;
- attempt to raise or protect the weapon without acquiring a stable sight picture;
- display impaired balance, blinking, ringing ears, panic, and delayed recognition.

Spook closes before the manager can deliver effective fire after the detonation.

DISARM

Spook reaches the manager, strips the handgun from him, and retains possession of it.

The disarm must visibly establish:

- the manager no longer controls the weapon;
- the handgun does not fall loose near the desk;
- Spook has positive possession before transitioning into the tackle;
- the manager remains alive.

TAKEDOWN TO THE FLOOR

Spook tackles the manager to the office floor and pins him down.

- The manager lands hard enough to lose resistance and breath but not so violently
  that the scene becomes gratuitous punishment.
- Spook controls the manager's torso and movement from above or behind.
- Spook presses the manager's own handgun muzzle into his back.
- The camera remains in close first-person perspective.
- The manager can look back over his shoulder toward Spook while pinned.

ALERTED PERFORMANCE STATE

Compared with Variant A, the manager is:

- more agitated;
- more frightened;
- more breathless;
- affected by the lingering stun-grenade disorientation;
- uncomfortable from the tackle and floor restraint;
- less capable of sustained speech without catching his breath.

The performance must remain intelligible through subtitles and dynamic audio mixing.

================================================================================
ALERTED INTERROGATION DIALOGUE
================================================================================

OPENING LINE

After catching enough breath to speak, the manager looks over his shoulder at Spook
and stammers:

    MANAGER
    I don't know what this is about, but take everything you want. I got money in
    the safe over there. I got nothing else of value here, anyway. You can have it.
    Just take it and get out. The police are on their way.

The delivery should be fragmented by breath, panic, and residual disorientation
without changing the locked wording.

AIRCRAFT DEMAND

Spook answers with the same core line used in Variant A:

    SPOOK
    I need a plane. One that I can use. Now!

The manager looks momentarily bewildered for approximately half a second, again
struggling to reconcile an aircraft demand with his assumption that this is a robbery.
Fear then reasserts itself and he replies:

    MANAGER
    OK. Yeah, OK... I keep one fuelled up for emergencies. Out on the jetty past the
    fuel tanks.

The same exact aircraft-answer wording is canonical in both variants.

ALERTED KEY DEMAND

Spook demands the keys more urgently than in Variant A:

    SPOOK
    The keys. Hand them over!

As he speaks, Spook makes a small forceful jab with the handgun muzzle against the
manager's back.

The jab is a brief coercive emphasis, not repeated torture or an extended beating.

MANAGER KEY RESPONSE

With audible panic and discomfort, the manager replies:

    MANAGER
    OK, OK, easy! Just take them. They're in my left pocket. In my jacket.

The manager does not attempt to reach for the keys himself while pinned.

================================================================================
PLAYER SEARCH AND KEY RECOVERY
================================================================================

SEARCH PROMPT

After the manager identifies the key location, the player receives:

    SEARCH

The prompt targets the manager's left jacket pocket rather than the desk or floor.

FIRST-PERSON SEARCH ANIMATION

Activating SEARCH triggers a short first-person animation in which Spook:

- keeps the manager pinned;
- maintains control of the manager's handgun;
- reaches to the manager's left jacket pocket;
- opens or pulls the pocket as required by the costume;
- retrieves the Found FBA-2C keyring;
- visibly confirms possession.

The animation must not imply that the manager is temporarily released or able to
roll toward another weapon.

KEY FAIL-SAFES

- The keys remain recoverable if cloth or body physics misalign the pocket.
- The SEARCH interaction cannot select the wrong side of the jacket.
- If the manager is moved slightly during the tackle, the interaction volume follows
  the correct pocket.
- Checkpoints cannot preserve a state in which the manager has surrendered the key
  location but SEARCH is unavailable.
- If the manager is accidentally incapacitated, the keys remain physically searchable
  so the mission does not softlock, although the intended authored outcome keeps him
  alive.

================================================================================
IMMEDIATE KEY TOSS TO SMOKE
================================================================================

SMOKE'S COVER POSITION

By the time Spook retrieves the keys, Smoke has moved to a position just inside the
broken office doorway and covers outward into the first-floor corridor.

He remains near enough to receive the keys without abandoning forward security.

SCRIPTED LOOK AND TOSS

Immediately after SEARCH completes:

- Spook's view pivots briefly toward Smoke;
- Spook calls:

    SPOOK
    Smoke! Catch!

- Spook immediately tosses the Found FBA-2C keyring across the short distance;
- Smoke catches the keys in mid-air;
- Smoke secures them in his own pocket or designated pilot-access location;
- the camera returns promptly to the manager and Tripper.

The toss should span only a few feet. It is a confident practical handoff, not a
showy trick shot.

KEY-OWNERSHIP STATE

Variant B key ownership progresses as follows:

    MANAGER'S LEFT JACKET POCKET
    -> SPOOK AFTER SEARCH
    -> SMOKE AFTER CATCH

Smoke becomes the operational keyholder and pilot-ready character as soon as the
catch completes.

There is no later office-doorway key-handover animation in normal live-manager Variant
B because that function has already been completed inside the room.

If the manager is dead before key recovery, the dedicated fallback instead uses a
body SEARCH followed by the Variant A direct doorway handover.

INTERRUPTION FAIL-SAFE

If an immediate corridor threat prevents the toss:

- combat control takes priority;
- Spook retains the keys temporarily;
- the handoff retries at the next safe short-range opportunity;
- the keys cannot duplicate, disappear, or be considered in Smoke's inventory before
  the catch or fallback transfer completes.

================================================================================
TRIPPER RESTRAINT AND OFFICE CLOSEOUT
================================================================================

CAMERA RETURN TO TRIPPER

After Smoke secures the keys, Spook's view turns briefly toward Tripper, who is
standing over or beside Spook and the tackled manager.

Spook motions for Tripper to secure the manager.

DESK RESTRAINT

Tripper takes physical control as Spook releases the pin and uses zip cuffs to secure
the manager to the adjacent desk.

The final restraint must:

- anchor the manager to a credible structural part of the desk;
- prevent him from reaching the office doorway, phone, loose equipment, or another
  weapon;
- keep him alive;
- remain visibly distinct from Variant A's chair restraint;
- leave the player's exit route clear.

If the floor plan requires a desk leg, cross-brace, or other fixed element, production
must choose an anchor that cannot be moved trivially by physics.

WEAPON SECURITY

Spook retains the manager's handgun after the disarm.

Before departure:

- the handgun is not returned to the manager;
- it is not dropped on the floor or desk;
- no other loose firearm remains within the restrained manager's reach;
- any weapon introduced into the room by physics, a fallen guard, or the player's
  earlier fire must be secured or made inaccessible;
- Tripper's breaching shotgun returns to controlled carriage after it is no longer
  needed.

The manager is left restrained, disarmed, and without access to a recoverable weapon.

================================================================================
OFFICE EXIT ORDER
================================================================================

SPOOK EXIT

Once Tripper has taken control of the manager and Smoke has secured the keys, normal

Spook moves toward the broken office doorway while Smoke continues covering the

SMOKE FOLLOW

Spook passes through the doorway first. Smoke then follows, transitioning his cover

TRIPPER FOLLOW

The alerted office-exit order is therefore:

1. SPOOK
2. SMOKE
3. TRIPPER

No key handover occurs during this exit in normal live-manager Variant B because
