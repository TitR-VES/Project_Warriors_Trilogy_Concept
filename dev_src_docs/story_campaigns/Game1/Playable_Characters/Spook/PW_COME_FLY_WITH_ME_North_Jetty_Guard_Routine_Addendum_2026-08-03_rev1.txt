================================================================================
NORTH-JETTY ROUTINE EXTENSION
================================================================================

WESTERN-EDGE PAUSE

In addition to his established patrol around the north jetty and aircraft berths,
the north-jetty contractor occasionally walks to the western edge of the jetty and
urinates into the ocean below.

The authored baseline is approximately forty-five seconds from settling into the
position through completion and return to normal patrol behavior.

During this interval:

- the guard faces outward toward the water;
- his visual attention is directed away from most of the jetty deck;
- his posture and occupied hands make him slower to react than during normal patrol;
- he remains capable of hearing nearby noise and reacting if clearly alerted;
- his radio remains on his person and can still become relevant if he detects the
  team before being controlled;
- the routine creates an opportunity rather than guaranteeing a free elimination.

The behavior should read as an unsanctioned private lapse by a complacent contractor,
not as slapstick staging or a prolonged visual gag.

================================================================================
TACTICAL OPPORTUNITY
================================================================================

STEALTH APPROACH

The western-edge pause gives a patient stealth player a temporary approach window
from the guard's rear or blind-side jetty lane.

Valid player responses remain consistent with the mission's broader force rules:

- bypass the guard while his attention is directed toward the water;
- close distance and perform a quiet non-lethal incapacitation;
- restrain him if the encounter state and animation space support it;
- perform a stealth kill;
- use another grounded distraction or takedown supported by the level geometry.

The game must not make killing the guard mandatory merely because the routine creates
a favorable lethal opportunity.

OCEAN BODY DISPOSAL

If the player performs a compatible close-quarters lethal takedown at the western
edge, the geometry may support immediately pushing, tipping, or carrying the body
over the edge into the water below.

This functions as an optional stealth body-disposal maneuver:

- the body leaves the visible jetty deck and normal land-based discovery routes;
- the action requires the player to be close enough to the western edge and aligned
  with a valid contextual animation volume;
- the takedown and disposal may be presented as one continuous contextual sequence
  or as a takedown followed by a separate body-interaction prompt;
- the player must retain enough situational vulnerability that the maneuver does not
  become a consequence-free automatic solution;
- nearby guards may still hear a struggle, impact, splash, dropped equipment, or
  other poorly controlled noise;
- the guard's radio and weapon must not remain floating or lying conspicuously on the
  jetty without an authored state;
- water disposal removes or reduces ordinary visual body discovery but does not erase
  prior alerts, witnessed behavior, radio calls, or other evidence already generated.

A non-lethal takedown must not automatically result in the unconscious guard being
thrown into deep water. Water disposal is reserved for a lethal outcome unless the
source later defines a safe, restrained alternative that does not imply accidental
drowning.

================================================================================
READABILITY AND FAIRNESS
================================================================================

The routine should be learnable through observation rather than hidden trial and
error.

Possible readable cues include:

- the guard breaking from his normal berth patrol and walking deliberately toward the
  western edge;
- a brief preparatory glance to confirm he believes he is unobserved;
- a consistent stopping point and outward-facing posture;
- companion observation dialogue or a surveillance tag if the player previously
  studied the base long enough;
- a recognizable return-to-patrol animation before his normal sightline resumes.

The routine interval should occur often enough to be tactically meaningful but not so
frequently that the guard appears implausibly occupied for much of his shift.
Production may vary the wait between occurrences within a bounded schedule to avoid
mechanical repetition, while preserving the approximately forty-five-second authored
vulnerability window once the behavior begins.

DIFFICULTY SCALING

Difficulty tuning may adjust:

- the delay before the routine first becomes available;
- the variability between occurrences;
- hearing sensitivity during the pause;
- the speed with which the guard aborts the behavior after detecting something;
- the amount of approach cover available near the western jetty edge.

Difficulty must not reduce the authored forty-five-second baseline so severely that
the behavior ceases to function as a deliberate stealth opportunity.

================================================================================
ALERT AND INTERRUPTION BEHAVIOR
================================================================================

The guard does not begin or continue the routine under an active compound alert.

- A completed radio alert, fixed-alarm activation, clearly audible gunfight, confirmed
  body discovery, or direct visual detection aborts the routine.
- On interruption, the guard turns back toward the jetty, attempts to regain his
  weapon-ready posture, and follows the controlling security-state logic.
- He does not remain artificially locked in the animation while combat develops.
- If the player initiates a takedown during the transition out of the routine, the
  animation system must choose a valid contextual response rather than allowing model
  overlap or invulnerability.
- The routine must not override his need to react to the Found FBA-2C engine starting,
  mooring activity, or other major departure cues.
