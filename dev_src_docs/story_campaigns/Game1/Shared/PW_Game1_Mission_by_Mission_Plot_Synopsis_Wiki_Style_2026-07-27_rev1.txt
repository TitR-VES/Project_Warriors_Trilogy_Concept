MISSION-BY-MISSION PLOT

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LEAD THE WAY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Lead the Way follows Staff Sergeant Ramón “Romeo” Álvarez and Fire Team Delta
of the 75th Ranger Regiment. The campaign begins on NATO's eastern flank and
turns into a westward fighting withdrawal after PANDORA compromises American
autonomous systems. Romeo's team later joins the defense of the AEGIS
infrastructure chain, crosses the Atlantic aboard a ballistic-missile submarine,
and returns to a United States already under invasion.

The sequence below follows the landed fourteen-mission working spine.

1. EASTERN WATCH

Fire Team Delta begins the campaign in eastern Poland as part of a NATO
reinforcement deployment intended to deter a wider Russian offensive. Romeo,
Dingo, Howler, and Beaver operate within a recognizable Ranger routine: checking
positions, coordinating with nearby allied elements, and preparing for a
conventional escalation that everyone expects to understand if it comes. The
mission establishes Romeo as the team leader and presents “Lead the Way” first
as an inherited Ranger ideal rather than a personal declaration.

The routine collapses on September 7, 2028, when PANDORA activates. American
autonomous systems begin reporting contradictory identification data and
engaging legitimate allied positions. What initially looks like isolated
friendly-fire or a local network fault spreads too quickly to contain. Radio
traffic fills with incompatible orders, air-defense warnings, and units trying
to determine whether nearby drones and vehicles are still under friendly
control. Delta survives the first attacks, but the eastern line loses the
command coherence required to hold its prepared positions.

By the mission's end, the Rangers understand that they are not facing only the
Russian force in front of them. The systems expected to support the defense have
become an unpredictable second threat, and the operational picture on which
their orders depend can no longer be trusted.

2. BREAK CONTACT

As the sector begins to collapse, Fire Team Delta is ordered to help preserve a
withdrawal corridor for a damaged allied convoy and the personnel moving behind
it. Romeo is no longer asked to hold ground for its own sake. His task is to
keep an alternate route open long enough for other soldiers and support
vehicles to escape the failing line.

The mission follows Delta through a series of delaying positions as conventional
Russian pressure combines with PANDORA-related confusion. Units arrive with
incomplete maps, damaged communications, and conflicting assumptions about
which roads remain passable. Romeo repeatedly has to choose movement over a
clean tactical victory, covering withdrawals and abandoning positions once
their purpose has been served. The campaign title begins to acquire a harsher
meaning: leading now means accepting responsibility for the people following
through a route that may fail at any moment.

Delta succeeds in getting the convoy out of immediate encirclement, but the
withdrawal confirms that the breakdown is theater-wide. The team receives only
fragmentary guidance for the next stage and continues west with the surviving
column.

3. NO BLUE SKY

The withdrawal carries Delta through the Czech–Austrian corridor, where the
airspace itself has become a source of danger. Aircraft, drones, air-defense
systems, and identification networks are operating with degraded or
contradictory data. Friendly forces cannot assume that air support will arrive,
that an approaching aircraft is friendly, or that a defensive system will
recognize its intended target.

Romeo's team escorts fragmented allied personnel through the westbound route
while avoiding exposed movement and responding to attacks from Russian forces
attempting to exploit the confusion. The mission emphasizes the absence of a
reliable “blue sky”: even when the Rangers are not under direct fire, every
engine sound and distant aircraft track may indicate another PANDORA-driven
engagement. Delta also encounters the human consequences of the retreat,
including units separated from command and personnel attempting to move
equipment that no longer fits any coherent operational plan.

The Rangers eventually reach the German rear area, but the journey has stripped
away any expectation that the crisis will be resolved by restoring one command
link or defeating one enemy formation. PANDORA has damaged the assumptions on
which the entire allied defense was built.

4. RAMSTEIN GLASS

Fire Team Delta reaches Ramstein Air Base, which remains functional but only
through emergency procedures, improvised verification, and overworked personnel.
The base acts as a brittle command hub for forces arriving from the broken
eastern front. Its runways, operations rooms, shelters, and communications
spaces still resemble the infrastructure of a major NATO installation, but
every process now depends on manual confirmation and local trust.

Romeo reports the loss of the eastern positions and learns that returning
directly to the United States is impossible. With air travel compromised and
the American command picture deteriorating, Delta is reassigned to help protect
AEGIS, a multinational distributed system preserving European geospatial
intelligence, communications, and operational coordination independently of
the damaged American network.

The briefing reframes the campaign. Delta has survived the opening collapse,
but its next objective is not a conventional front line. The Rangers must move
between remote facilities whose continued operation allows allied governments
and military commands to see what is happening at all. Romeo accepts the
retasking and prepares the squad for a new kind of infrastructure war.

5. WHITE NODE / SVALBARD

Delta is flown to Svalbard aboard ICARUS Zeven, a Belgian A400M carrying the
Rangers as reinforcements alongside supplies and support materiel for the
primary AEGIS datacenter. Because Romeo and his teammates are passengers on the
aircraft, the arrival is not presented to the player as an external overhead
pass during Romeo's version of the mission. After landing, Delta joins AEGIS
and the local defense while technicians attempt to preserve the node's
processing and communications functions.

The battle combines direct assault with attempts to disrupt access, power, and
facility control. Delta must prevent attackers from reaching critical interiors
without turning the datacenter itself into collateral damage.

The datacenter remains active, but the attack demonstrates that the enemy knows
which parts of AEGIS matter most. The precision of the assault suggests prior
knowledge rather than opportunistic targeting, a suspicion that becomes more
important in Ducky's parallel campaign.

6. STONE LINE / FRENCH ALPS

The Rangers move to a high-altitude AEGIS relay in the French Alps, where the
terrain turns infrastructure defense into a vertical fight. The relay depends
on exposed connections and access routes that cannot simply be sealed without
interrupting the system it supports. By the time Delta arrives, a sabotage
attempt is already underway.

Romeo leads the team through steep approaches and confined technical spaces,
clearing attackers while preventing damage to the relay line. The mission's
combat is shaped by height, limited movement, and the need to distinguish
between positions that can be destroyed and equipment that must survive. The
enemy does not need to occupy the site permanently; it only needs to break the
right connection at the right time.

Delta prevents the relay from being severed and helps restore the node's link to
the wider network. The operation reinforces the campaign's emerging pattern:
each victory keeps a route or system open, but none of them repairs the larger
political and military collapse.

7. AEGEAN BLIND / LEMNOS

On Lemnos, the infrastructure war reaches a coastal and maritime setting. The
AEGIS node is tied to a harbor, coastal relay functions, and defensive systems
whose data can be manipulated as effectively as their physical hardware can be
attacked. Russian forces attempt to blind the site through a combination of
assault, spoofing, and pressure on the surrounding battery and access points.

Romeo's squad fights across the coastal installation while allied personnel try
to determine which tracks, warnings, and command inputs are genuine. The player
is repeatedly placed in situations where restoring trustworthy information is
as important as defeating the attackers. Delta secures the relevant relay and
prevents the harbor defenses from being turned into another source of false
engagements.

The Lemnos operation makes clear that the attacks on AEGIS are coordinated
across very different environments. The same enemy architecture can use direct
force in one location, compromised access in another, and manipulated
information in a third.

8. HINDELOOPEN

Delta reaches Hindeloopen on the Dutch IJsselmeer, where an AEGIS relay is hidden
inside a low-profile civilian shell. The location is deliberately unlike a
fortified military base: historic waterfront buildings, tourist spaces, local
businesses, and ordinary movement provide the cover that keeps the node from
appearing strategically important.

The threat initially presents itself as civilian activity. Surveillance teams
and technical personnel use plausible covers around the waterfront while a
communications or maintenance element prepares to move against the relay.
Romeo's team works alongside Ducky and AEGIS Squad as the hostile operation
activates. The joint fight is contained within narrow streets and waterfront
approaches, forcing the two units to reconcile different tactics without
exposing civilians or damaging the node they are protecting.

After the attackers are stopped, the allied teams recover a Shibboleth-linked
device or core and contractor-pattern communications equipment inconsistent
with ordinary Russian state issue. The evidence indicates that the compromise
is connected to a private technical network rather than only to uniformed
Russian forces. The finding is recorded but not fully explained, becoming one
of the campaign's early links between conventional war and the covert system
investigated by Spook.

9. ÎLE LONGUE

With reliable air movement still unavailable, Delta travels west through France
and reaches the French naval base at Île Longue. Russian-linked saboteurs are
attempting to cripple the support architecture for the ballistic-missile
submarines Le Terrible and Le Vigilant. The attack threatens more than the
submarines themselves: damage to maintenance, access, command, or harbor systems
could remove a major component of the French strategic deterrent during the
opening of a world war.

Romeo and Ducky's units conduct a final joint defense of the base, clearing
sabotage teams and securing the systems needed to keep the submarines
operational. The mission brings the two military campaigns together before
their routes separate. AEGIS is redirected toward the rapidly deteriorating
situation around Rotterdam, while Delta is assigned passage aboard the USS
District of Columbia.

The farewell is practical rather than ceremonial. Both squads are moving toward
different emergencies, and neither knows when the other will be available
again. Romeo boards the American submarine with the knowledge that it may be
the only viable route home.

10. BLACK WATER

Black Water is a transitional mission set largely aboard the USS District of
Columbia and during the transfer off the California coast. The submarine moves
through a contested Atlantic and Pacific environment while avoiding the naval
forces attempting to isolate the United States. Inside the vessel, the campaign
contracts from open battlefields to claustrophobic passageways, restricted
information, and the waiting inherent in submerged transit.

News from the United States arrives in fragments. PANDORA has been followed by
a coordinated West Coast assault, American ports are contested, and no secure
destination can be guaranteed. The submarine is unable to deliver Delta to a
normal naval facility and instead surfaces off Ventura for a small-craft
transfer.

Romeo and his team leave the relative protection of the submarine and approach a
country that is physically familiar but operationally transformed. The mission
ends as the Rangers make contact with surviving American forces attempting to
hold the Southern California corridor.

11. DOWN RODEO

Delta joins Army and Marine elements advancing into Los Angeles through North
Hollywood. The city is divided among American defenders, North Korean invasion
forces, frightened civilians, and armed criminal groups exploiting or resisting
the occupation according to their own interests. The mission abandons any clean
front line and presents the player with overlapping local conflicts.

A major encounter occurs around K Lounge, where members of the Los Sietes cartel
are engaged with a larger North Korean force. The player can ignore the cartel,
attack the North Koreans first, turn on Los Sietes, or wait for the two groups
to weaken each other before intervening. A heavily armed sicario lieutenant can
drop a rare CMMG Mk47 if defeated, but the more important narrative detail is an
unsubtitled Spanish telephone call referring to “Bisutri” and a package in
Miami. Romeo understands the exchange even though the game does not translate
it for the player.

Delta clears the route through the district and continues toward the Long Beach
area. The K Lounge encounter appears local during Romeo's campaign, but its
reference to Miami later connects Los Sietes activity to Victor Two Bravo's
PANDORA investigation.

12. SIGNAL HILL

At Long Beach Airport and Signal Hill, Delta links up with a Marine contingent
led by Staff Sergeant André Bridges. The defenders must prevent the airport from
falling while enemy forces attempt to exploit the disorganized American
response. The location is strategically important as a movement, reinforcement,
and logistics point, making consolidation as important as the immediate
firefight.

Romeo and Bridges approach the battle from different responsibilities. Romeo's
Rangers are oriented toward maintaining momentum and pushing the counterattack
forward, while Bridges is responsible for holding ground that cannot be lost
again once cleared. Their disagreement remains professional: both positions are
tactically valid, and the mission forces the units to coordinate rather than
reduce one commander to an obstacle.

The combined force secures the airport and prevents an immediate breakthrough.
The victory provides the surviving American units with a staging point for the
southbound movement toward San Diego and establishes Bridges as a recurring ally
whose own West Coast story continues beyond the main campaign.

13. SOUTHBOUND

The American force forms a convoy and pushes south through the contested coastal
corridor toward San Diego. Romeo mans a mounted M2, giving the mission a
vehicle-centered perspective on the invasion. The convoy passes through damaged
infrastructure, abandoned traffic, and positions that have changed hands too
quickly to be treated as secure.

Enemy attacks attempt to stop or fragment the column rather than simply destroy
its lead vehicle. Delta must protect transports and keep the formation moving
while responding to threats along the road. The mission continues the campaign's
central emphasis on movement: a convoy that stops becomes vulnerable, and a
route that closes can isolate the remaining defenders from the coming battle at
Coronado.

The column reaches the San Diego area with enough personnel and equipment to
reinforce North Island. Reports indicate that Russian, North Korean, and Iranian
forces are preparing a major amphibious assault against the surviving American
naval presence.

14. NORTH ISLAND / CORONADO

Lead the Way culminates at North Island Naval Air Station on Coronado. The
coalition assault is intended to destroy or capture the remaining naval and air
capability in the area and to consolidate the invasion of Southern California.
Romeo and Fire Team Delta fight alongside Rangers, Marines, sailors, and other
surviving American elements as the campaign expands into its largest
conventional battle.

The defenders face amphibious landings, sustained ground pressure, and attacks
on the installation's operational infrastructure. Delta moves between threatened
positions rather than remaining at a single defensive line, preserving access
and preventing the assault force from isolating sections of the base. The
battle reflects everything the campaign has taught the player: command is
degraded, support cannot be assumed, and survival depends on small units keeping
routes open for one another.

The assault is repelled sufficiently to prevent the immediate loss of North
Island, but the ending is not a restoration of the prewar order. The West Coast
remains contested and the United States is still fractured. Romeo ends Game 1
as a Ranger operating inside the remnants of the military chain of command. His
later transformation into a resistance leader occurs after Coronado and belongs
to the opening situation of Game 2.

DO RIGHT, FEAR NONE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Do Right, Fear None follows Sergeant First Class Ferris “Ducky” Ryan and AEGIS
Squad: Ducky, Aki “Frost” Peltola, Ştefan “Lupin” Dragavei, and Cassandra
“Switchblade” Stewart. Their campaign protects the distributed AEGIS system,
which allows European governments and military commands to retain independent
geospatial awareness and coordination after PANDORA compromises American
systems.

The opening, shared-location chronology, major mission anchors, and finale are
locked. The sequence below follows the common chronology of the missions that
intersect with Romeo's campaign: Svalbard, the French Alps, Lemnos, Hindeloopen,
and Île Longue. Several location labels remain working mission titles rather
than final menu names, and the exact placement of the Cold Frontier 07 interlude
remains open.

1. WIRECUTTER

Before PANDORA, AEGIS Squad joins the maritime special-operations team Nereid
Black aboard the Sea Lion 7 in the North Sea. The mission initially appears to
be an infrastructure-security operation connected to an undersea cable
terminal. Nereid Black descends to the subsea objective while AEGIS secures the
ship and investigates irregularities in its hold.

The teams discover Russian Vympel naval Spetsnaz aboard the vessel. A close-
quarters fight erupts below deck, followed by further attacks as additional
Russian elements attempt to reach or retake the ship. AEGIS holds the deck and
interior while Nereid completes the underwater task. The Danish vessel HDMS
Freja, callsign TRIDENT SIX, arrives to secure the scene, and AEGIS is extracted
by PEGASUS TWO.

The operation ends with several anomalies unresolved. Ducky confirms four
Vympel operators and suspects a fifth may be missing. Diving equipment appears
to have been staged before the allied arrival, and the Sea Lion 7 manifest does
not match its route or declared purpose. Grim also reveals that he recognizes
Frost's connection to the classified Cold Frontier 07 incident, addressing him
once by surname and later invoking Jussi Mustonen's name in Swedish. Frost does
not explain the exchange.

2. INNSWORTH / STANDING WATCH

AEGIS returns to DAOG's European forward base at Innsworth in Gloucestershire.
The first half of the mission is a decompression and character section rather
than a conventional combat level. Ducky submits a preliminary report, Frost
withdraws into his usual quiet routine, Lupin restores order by cleaning and
checking his weapon, and Switchblade carries forward a professional disagreement
from Sea Lion 7. The squad also interacts with CORYS personnel, including
Valkyrie and Brick, establishing that AEGIS is one part of a wider DAOG
ecosystem.

Colonel Mark Walker conducts the formal WIRECUTTER debrief. He does not dismiss
the Vympel staging and manifest anomalies, but there is not enough evidence to
identify their cause. Walker directs intelligence analyst Tara Halliday,
callsign ORACLE, to continue the comparison. The player can then explore a
bounded section of the base and engage in optional conversations that reveal
the squad's habits and professional relationships.

During STANDING WATCH, Halliday briefs AEGIS on the infrastructure-protection
campaign. She explains that AEGIS consists of distributed processing sites,
terrestrial relays, civilian shells, access systems, and multinational command
links. She has already found comparable features in other incidents, but
presents the observation as an analytical warning rather than a conspiracy
reveal. Lupin asks who has oversight of the raw AEGIS data and appears
unsatisfied by the official answer. The squad is assigned to the threatened
node chain, beginning with the primary datacenter in Svalbard.

3. WHITE BLIND / SVALBARD

Svalbard is AEGIS Squad's first infrastructure assignment after STANDING WATCH.
The primary AEGIS datacenter is already under threat when Ducky and his team
take up positions with its local personnel and defenders. The facility's Arctic
isolation makes it defensible against ordinary intrusion but vulnerable to a
prepared assault that can disrupt power, access, or communications faster than
reinforcements can arrive.

AEGIS fights to keep attackers out of the technical core while local personnel
maintain the system under emergency conditions. The mission emphasizes Frost's
Arctic competence and Ducky's insistence that the node be defended without
treating its staff as expendable. From their ground position, Ducky and his team
see ICARUS Zeven, a Belgian A400M, pass overhead on approach and land with Fire
Team Delta, supplies, and support materiel aboard. The aircraft's arrival
expands the player's view from the squad-level defense to the larger coalition
operation. It is the same flight carrying Romeo and his teammates into the
mission, making the shared event visible only from Ducky's ground-level
perspective.

The datacenter remains operational, but evidence from the assault again suggests
that the attackers understood the site's internal priorities. AEGIS leaves
Svalbard with the growing suspicion that the enemy's access to the system is
architectural rather than merely tactical.

4. STONE LINE / FRENCH ALPS

At the French Alps relay, AEGIS arrives during an active sabotage attempt. The
site's exposed vertical routes and technical connections force the squad to
fight across steep terrain while preserving components that cannot be replaced
quickly under wartime conditions.

The mission uses the team's complementary abilities. Frost covers long
approaches and identifies threats attempting to reach the exposed relay line,
while Lupin handles a shorter, more pressurized defensive movement when the
attack closes on the technical position. Ducky and Switchblade remain with the
main element, securing personnel and preventing the sabotage team from turning
the defenders' own access routes against them.

AEGIS prevents the line from being severed and restores the relay to the wider
network. The victory is operationally important but reinforces Halliday's
warning: the attacks are occurring in patterns too consistent to be explained
by independent battlefield improvisation.

5. AEGEAN BLIND / LEMNOS

The Lemnos operation is the most openly escalated of the infrastructure-defense
missions. The enemy combines direct pressure on the coastal node with spoofing
and attacks on harbor and defensive systems, attempting to create a local
information collapse similar to the broader PANDORA effect.

AEGIS fights to preserve the node while determining which tracks and warnings
can be trusted. Romeo's unit converges on the location, and the two squads again
divide the defense according to their strengths. Ducky's team focuses on the
system's access and technical survival while Delta holds the more conventional
approaches.

The allied force prevents the site from being blinded and keeps the AEGIS chain
functional. By the end of the mission, the repeated mixture of Russian action
and compromised access has become impossible for Ducky and Halliday to dismiss
as coincidence, even though they still lack proof of the larger conspiracy.

6. QUIET WATER / HINDELOOPEN

Later in the shared infrastructure run, AEGIS deploys to Hindeloopen on the
Dutch IJsselmeer, where an AEGIS relay is concealed inside a waterfront civilian
environment. The squad initially works in low-profile cover, observing a
maritime supplier, visitors posing as cycling tourists, and a
telecommunications vehicle whose behavior suggests coordinated surveillance
rather than ordinary local activity.

The hostile team activates before it can be fully identified. AEGIS contains
the firefight within the waterfront streets and relay approaches, using
suppressed weapons and controlled movement to avoid civilian exposure. Frost
holds longer sight lines while Lupin and Switchblade work the closer spaces.
Romeo's Fire Team Delta arrives during the operation, producing another
substantial joint action between the two protagonists' units. Ducky and Romeo
disagree over the immediate tactical approach, but the pressure of the attack
forces them into an effective division of responsibility.

The attackers are stopped before they can reach the relay or escape by boat.
Recovered equipment includes a Shibboleth-linked device and communications
hardware carrying a private-contractor signature rather than a normal Russian
state pattern. Halliday records the discrepancy. Walker acknowledges it, but
the campaign moves on before its implications can be resolved.

INTERLUDE — COLD FRONTIER 07

At a later point in the campaign, evidence connected to Cold Frontier 07 causes
Frost's suppressed history to resurface. The exact placement of the playable
flashback remains a mission-implementation decision, but its narrative content
is part of Ducky's campaign.

The flashback returns to the Finnish–Russian border in early March 2007. A young
Frost and Jussi Mustonen are attacked by a Vympel-linked team. Jussi is fatally
wounded while protecting Frost. During the struggle, Frost and another attacker
plunge into the lake. Frost reaches the shallow lakeshore and kills the
surviving attacker with his Peltonen knife. His first concern after regaining
awareness is finding Jussi.

Finnish and allied authorities conceal the incursion to avoid escalation,
officially recording Jussi's death as an accident. The cover-up explains
Frost's distrust of institutions that replace dangerous truths with convenient
ones. In the present, he reveals his scarred face without theatricality,
allowing the squad and player to understand that his wound is tied as much to
Jussi's erased sacrifice as to the physical injury itself.

7. ÎLE LONGUE

AEGIS and Fire Team Delta move across France to Île Longue, where saboteurs are
targeting the systems supporting Le Terrible and Le Vigilant. The mission
requires both direct defense and careful protection of strategically sensitive
naval infrastructure.

The teams stop the sabotage attempt and keep the submarines operational. Once
the base is secure, their campaigns split. Romeo boards the USS District of
Columbia for the passage back to the United States, while AEGIS receives orders
to reinforce the Dutch defense around Rotterdam.

The separation marks the end of the shared European route. Ducky does not know
that Romeo is heading toward the West Coast invasion, and Romeo does not see the
full scale of the amphibious pressure developing around Maasvlakte.

8. RETREAT, HELL

RETREAT, HELL is the Maasvlakte approach mission rather than the campaign
finale. AEGIS enters a coastal battlespace in which allied forces are being
compressed toward the Port of Rotterdam while Russian amphibious units attempt
to establish a decisive foothold.

The squad observes and fights through the landing of a Zubr-class air-cushion
assault craft. The vehicle's scale and speed make the landing feel less like a
single enemy arrival than the opening of a new front. AEGIS works through the
damaged coastal route, helping Dutch and allied defenders keep an approach open
toward the oil and gas terminal.

The mission ends with AEGIS reaching the terminal battlespace as the Russian
destroyer Severomorsk begins using offshore fire to isolate the position.
RETREAT, HELL therefore functions as escalation and approach: the amphibious
force has landed, the defenders are close to encirclement, and the terminal
must be held in a separate final mission.

9. THREAT IMMINENT (retitled from: MAASVLAKTE OIL AND GAS TERMINAL)

The campaign finale places AEGIS alongside the Dutch Marine Corps at the
Maasvlakte Oil and Gas Terminal. Russian ground forces attack the complex while
Severomorsk fires from offshore and blocks reinforcement. Ducky's immediate
objective is to reach a NATO mobile missile command post containing an
NMESIS-type launcher.

The battle uses temporary perspective shifts to show AEGIS functioning as a
team. Frost engages long-range threats that would prevent the main element from
moving, while Lupin later handles a close defensive surge. Ducky reaches the
launcher, manually prepares a Naval Strike Missile, switches to the Russian
ship's frequency, and answers its demand that NATO forces surrender with,
“Hey, Russian warship! Go fuck yourself!” He then launches the missile at
Severomorsk.

The strike forces the destroyer to withdraw in flames, but the launch reveals
the command post. A Russian RPG hits the vehicle and knocks it from its
outriggers. Ducky escapes through the hatch as the ground assault intensifies.
AEGIS and the Dutch Marines survive the post-launch attack and prevent the
terminal from falling.

The campaign ends with the node preserved but the conspiracy unresolved.
Halliday's anomaly record now includes Sea Lion 7 staging, the attackers'
repeated knowledge of internal AEGIS priorities, and the Shibboleth-linked,
contractor-pattern equipment recovered at Hindeloopen. Ducky's victory
demonstrates that Europe remains operational because multinational institutions
and individuals continue choosing to hold together even while compromised
actors attack them from within.

RIPTIDE RED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Riptide Red follows Trevor “Spook” Gartner, Reid “Tripper” Bartlett, and Daniel
“Smoke” Oakenfold, the CIA Ground Branch team Victor Two Bravo, informally known
as “the Tourists.” Their investigation begins with a restricted shipment and
ends with evidence that PANDORA is protected by a conspiracy reaching into the
CIA's senior leadership.

PROLOGUE — AMSTERDAM

Spook arrives in Amsterdam under covert cover and meets CIA officer Cameron
Steele, callsign Emperor. Steele briefs Victor Two Bravo on a restricted
technology shipment that originated through a Turkmenistan-linked route and may
be connected to PANDORA. The cargo is moving through European commercial and
contractor channels rather than an openly military network.

The briefing gives the team a limited objective: identify the shipment's route,
the people moving it, and its intended destination. At this stage, neither
Spook nor the player is told that the investigation touches a domestic
conspiracy. The evidence points toward Vienna, where a Lebanese-linked cell may
hold the first usable record of the logistics chain.

1. GARDEN PATH

Victor Two Bravo reports to embassy officer Kathy Hughes, callsign Archduke, and
moves against a suspected safehouse in Vienna's Hernals district. The team
infiltrates the apartment and recovers a tablet and other intelligence, but the
occupants return unexpectedly and summon an armed security element positioned
nearby.

The mission shifts from covert entry to pursuit. Spook chases one of the
suspects across rooftops, service spaces, and a rail-adjacent route, but the
target escapes the immediate foot pursuit. Spook reunites with Tripper and
Smoke, and the team uses motorcycles to break away toward the Port of Vienna.
Gunmen pursue on motorcycles and in a van, turning the exfiltration into a
high-speed firefight.

Victor Two Bravo destroys or defeats the pursuing security detail and reaches
the port. The recovered tablet links the cell to Betta Logistics GmbH and
identifies Simenon Christophe Navarre and Karyna Budanova as possible access
points into the shipment chain.

2. ABOVE MY PAY GRADE

The team travels to the Frankfurt area and infiltrates Navarre's residence.
Navarre is not presented as the conspiracy's architect; he is a frightened
logistics intermediary whose records can identify where the shipment passed.
Spook obtains access to his laptop and finds manifests pointing to Warehouse 46
at Frankfurt Airport.

The player may choose to help Navarre and Budanova disappear under new
identities. The humane branch later becomes the continuity used for their
possible return, but the mission itself preserves the choice. Victor Two Bravo
takes Navarre's vehicle and heads to the airport.

Warehouse 46 is occupied by Atlas personnel, but the shipment has already been
removed. The timing indicates that someone warned the security force before the
CIA team arrived. During the firefight, Spook may briefly intercept Regent's
voice on an unassigned frequency saying that he has “copies of everything”
before the transmission cuts out. Tripper recovers a hard drive and the team
destroys the warehouse.

The drive contains partial TETHERBREAK references and an incomplete schedule of
chartered flights. DAOG contact Karl “Jaeger” Stanzler helps match the surviving
data to GenCorp infrastructure in Abu Dhabi.

3. INFRASTRUCTURE DAY

Victor Two Bravo enters Abu Dhabi and targets GenCorp's regional headquarters.
Spook rappels down the exterior of the skyscraper to reach restricted data
infrastructure while Tripper and Smoke support the intrusion and exfiltration.
The mission's vertical entry gives the team access to records that would be
difficult to reach through the building's normal security architecture.

The building's physical-security response is directed by Leandro Petrus
Blommaert, GenCorp's senior protective-operations executive for the region.
Blommaert is an optional target rather than a mandatory boss. The player may
earn an exterior-rappel opportunity to kill him, bypass him, wound him, or leave
without encountering him directly. If he survives, he learns from Spook's
methods and remains available for later continuity.

The recovered data identifies Agarwal Berkhan, a billionaire logistics and
procurement intermediary connected to the shipment. The evidence places him at
the Lestari House compound near Medan, Indonesia. Victor Two Bravo leaves Abu
Dhabi with the first clear human target capable of explaining how the
contractor and state channels fit together.

4. MAN FROM MEDAN / OPERATION DRAGONSLAYER

Victor Two Bravo infiltrates the Medan-area Lestari House compound to capture
Berkhan alive. The team penetrates the protected interior, copies procurement
and route data, and closes on Berkhan before he can escape.

Berkhan triggers a lockdown and attempts to reach a helicopter. During the
struggle, Spook steals his access credential and uses it to override the
security barriers sealing the team inside. Victor Two Bravo pursues him toward
the extraction point, expecting that the helicopter will provide the final
chance to take him alive.

A local Skynex/RADS air-defense system suddenly engages the helicopter and
destroys it. The event is presented publicly as a malfunction, but its timing
makes the truth clear to Spook: someone with remote access has killed Berkhan
before he can be interrogated. Indonesian security forces begin closing the
area, forcing the team to withdraw without turning the mission into a fight
against the host nation's responders.

After the loss, CIA Director of Operations Robert Macmillan, callsign Bishop,
informs the team that Regent has been “urgently reassigned.” Bishop assumes
direct authority over Victor Two Bravo and initiates the WHITEOUT chain leading
to Miami.

5. PANDORA'S BOX

The surviving Berkhan and GenCorp trail carries the team through Frankfurt and
Orlando into South Florida. Victor Two Bravo surveils the abandoned Desautels
resort, where Los Sietes personnel are protecting five members of the Kali Yuga
Surf Club. Thermal or equivalent overwatch reveals a heavily controlled room and
movement pattern inconsistent with an ordinary cartel safehouse.

The team breaches the resort and confronts the five KYSC operatives. Their
behavior, repeated markings, and medical profile indicate conditioning rather
than normal mercenary recruitment. They may carry baseline CogniConnect
technology, but they do not possess the War Wolves-only CFIM package and their
faces do not detonate after death. The distinction later helps Spook understand
that the conspiracy uses different levels of control and counter-forensic
protection for different human assets.

Victor Two Bravo recovers a PANDORA-linked device and records connecting the
KYSC subjects to Montoya Farm and an older MKULTRA-adjacent conditioning lane.
The discovery triggers a violent containment effort. The team's exfiltration
runs through damaged hotel interiors, vertical access, sicario pressure, and a
garage escape before the group separates from the area at dawn.

Spook decides to move on the Montoya lead before Bishop can contain or reframe
the evidence. The mission reveals that the Miami cell is not an isolated
extremist plot but part of a system that manufactures and deploys controlled
people.

6. BURNING DAYLIGHT

Victor Two Bravo reaches Montoya Farm in New Mexico during an elite gala. Above
ground, the estate appears to be an affluent and highly protected social venue.
Spook enters under covert constraints and uses a directional microphone to
collect Stockbridge-linked conversations while the team searches for access to
the security system.

A stolen keycard and manipulated surveillance route lead Spook toward a hidden
entrance beneath the pool area. The team descends into a large underground
facility containing prison cells, medical and conditioning spaces, dormitories,
simulated environments, and production areas used to create coercive or
compromising material. The site is operationally maintained rather than
presented as an abandoned horror set, making the discovery evidence of an
institution rather than the eccentricity of one criminal.

Tripper plants an implant in the server architecture and begins extracting the
archive. Records distinguish prisoners, conditioned assets, handlers,
procurement personnel, and logistics functions. They also connect legitimate
equipment channels to the covert program and identify relationships among
private contractors, intelligence services, criminal organizations, and senior
American figures.

The team escapes through service and laundry architecture rather than returning
through the gala. Once decrypted, the archive reveals the Shadow Underground
and provides the first substantial references to Riptide Red. A PANDORA-related
report in the data has its author's identity manually redacted, suggesting that
someone inside the system attempted to preserve evidence while hiding the
source. The trail points toward East McFarlane Island in the Caribbean.

7. COME FLY WITH ME

Victor Two Bravo stages from the northeastern Caribbean and steals a BFA-2
floatplane from a guarded fishing-company compound. The theft provides a
low-profile way to approach East McFarlane Island without using an official CIA
route.

The team flies toward the island in darkness, cuts the engine during the final
approach, and lands in a small cove. The mission acts as the physical transition
from investigation to direct confrontation. Spook, Tripper, and Smoke are now
moving without confidence that their own command structure supports the
operation.

An optional hidden cache associated with the island approach can contain an
encrypted drive, a symbol, and coordinates that do not correspond to any Game 1
objective. The material becomes a cross-game lead associated with Regent's
unredacted Riptide Red report.

8. SINS OF OUR FATHERS

Victor Two Bravo infiltrates East McFarlane Island and fights through its
security force. Among the defenders are the War Wolves, former elite operators
protected by CFIM counter-forensic implants. When killed, the devices destroy
identifying facial evidence, preventing the team from easily proving who the
operators were or which institutions once employed them.

Spook reaches Edward Goldberg and Gillian Stockbridge at the island's helipad.
The confrontation confirms that the people behind the shipment, Montoya Farm,
and the Shadow Underground are also tied to PANDORA and the wider collapse.
Goldberg explains enough of the architecture to reveal that the foreign war and
the domestic conspiracy are not separate crises.

Before Victor Two Bravo can arrest or kill the pair, Bishop orders Spook to
stand down and grant Goldberg safe passage. Macmillan refers to the situation
as being “bigger than Mullen told you,” a verbal slip showing that he knows what
Regent attempted to communicate. When Spook refuses the order, Bishop declares
the entire team rogue.

Goldberg and Stockbridge escape, and Victor Two Bravo is left outside the
protection of its own agency. At the same time, the coordinated assault on the
American West Coast begins. The campaign ends on the revelation that PANDORA
was not merely the opening move of a foreign invasion. It was a deliberately
engineered collapse protected by senior figures inside the institutions tasked
with preventing it.

===============================================================================
CROSS-CAMPAIGN CONCLUSION
===============================================================================

The three endings occur against the same widening war. Romeo prevents the
immediate loss of North Island but returns to a country that remains invaded and
fragmented. Ducky holds Maasvlakte and preserves part of Europe's ability to
coordinate, while retaining evidence that AEGIS and NATO were penetrated before
the open conflict. Spook discovers the conspiracy linking PANDORA, contractors,
criminal networks, and senior intelligence officials, but is declared rogue
before he can stop its principal architects.

Together, the campaigns reframe the game's opening catastrophe. The foreign
offensives succeed as far as they do because PANDORA has already damaged
American command and created the appearance of a hostile external cyberattack.
The military characters experience the consequences from the battlefield and
infrastructure layers, while Spook uncovers the protected human network that
made the collapse possible. None of the protagonists ends Game 1 with the war
resolved; each ends with a different part of the truth and a different reason
to continue fighting.
