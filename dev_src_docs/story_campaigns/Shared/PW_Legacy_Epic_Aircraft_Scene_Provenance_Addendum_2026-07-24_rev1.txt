PURPOSE
Record the original dramatic intent behind the early Gripen, A400M, AC-130, and A-10 scene drafts while preventing obsolete placements, callsigns, or speculative production detail from overriding later mission sources.

SHARED SCENE THESIS
The aircraft scenes exist to make the war briefly become much larger than the playable squad and then return the player to ground scale. They are not detached air-show cinematics. Each scene must:
- arise from a real tactical or logistical need;
- be perceived primarily from the ground/player position;
- use radio and sound to establish allied competence and multinational scale;
- give the player enough control to feel present without allowing the moment to be easily missed;
- resolve pressure or reveal scale, then return immediately to the mission.

PLAYER-ATTENTION RULE
Use authored gaze guidance sparingly:
- sound arrives first;
- squad body language, shadow, radio, or a light focus prompt can pull attention;
- never lock the camera for a long hero shot when a grounded glance can carry the same effect.

04. A-10 / THUNDERBOLT RESTORED-CAPABILITY SCENE
===============================================================================

Original identity
- Romeo-side Game 3 resistance force encounters an enemy armor/IFV column on a suburban/peripheral entry road rather than in dense urban core;
- the player has fought without reliable air support long enough for its return to matter;
- initial perception may be from inside/opening an APC or IFV hatch;
- engine whine and impacts precede full visual recognition;
- GAU-8 passes break the armor problem after ground forces have earned and held the strike window;
- ordinary American commercial/suburban architecture makes the restored allied capability feel uncanny and specific.

Protected dramatic rule
The A-10 strike is the resolution to a ground problem, not the whole mission. The player fights through escalating vehicle pressure and holds the line before the aircraft can work.

Technology/worldbuilding direction
The early draft used the A-10's relative mechanical simplicity and reduced dependence on fragile networked systems as an advantage in a PANDORA-degraded battlespace. Treat this as plausible thematic direction, not an absolute claim that the aircraft is electronics-free or invulnerable.

Placement rule
Current NO KINGS, BODYWORK, DAY WINDOW, and later A-10 sources control final placement. Do not automatically attach the scene to Van Hult/BODYWORK or another mission merely because that was explored in the early thread.
