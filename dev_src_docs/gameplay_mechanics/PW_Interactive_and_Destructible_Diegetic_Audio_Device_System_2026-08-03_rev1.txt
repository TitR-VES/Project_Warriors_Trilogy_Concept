PROJECT WARRIORS — GAMEPLAY MECHANICS
INTERACTIVE AND DESTRUCTIBLE DIEGETIC AUDIO DEVICE SYSTEM
Generated: 2026-08-03
Version: rev1
Status: SOURCE-READY GAME-WIDE SYSTEM LOCK

PURPOSE

Define the default game-world behavior for exposed radios, portable speakers, stereos,
televisions functioning as sound sources, compact sound systems, music players, and
similar ordinary audio-emitting devices.

The system exists to preserve physical consistency. A visibly ordinary radio should
not continue playing after it has been shot apart, blasted off a table, or deliberately
switched off by the player. At the same time, the rule must not force implausible
small-arms destruction onto reinforced public-address infrastructure, recessed or
concealed speakers, protected amplifiers, buried cabling, or other installation-grade
audio hardware.

This source establishes the reusable game-wide rule. Mission-specific sources may
narrow timing, prompt availability, persistence, or authored consequences, but should
not silently contradict the physical behavior defined here.

================================================================================
CORE RULE
================================================================================

Ordinary exposed audio devices are physical world objects.

Unless a controlling mission source explicitly establishes a hardened installation,
they should:

- respond to direct small-arms hits;
- respond to damaging nearby explosions and blast impulse;
- stop producing audio when their functional damage threshold is crossed;
- retain their resulting on, off, damaged, disabled, displaced, or destroyed state;
- expose a close-range interaction prompt when the player can plausibly operate them;
- never continue emitting clean music or speech from a visibly destroyed chassis.

This is a consistency rule, not a universal requirement that every incidental speaker
receive elaborate destruction simulation. Production may use authored damage states,
material swaps, particle effects, sound transitions, or a simpler disable event as
appropriate to asset importance and performance budget.

================================================================================
DEVICE CLASSES
================================================================================

CLASS A — ORDINARY EXPOSED DEVICES

Default interactable and destructible class.

Examples:

- tabletop radios;
- portable shortwave or AM/FM receivers;
- compact stereos and shelf systems;
- portable Bluetooth-type speakers;
- boomboxes;
- exposed desktop speakers;
- televisions or monitors currently serving as an audible media source;
- exposed music players connected to a local speaker;
- ordinary office, domestic, workshop, barracks, shop, or vehicle audio units.

Expected behavior:

- player operation when controls are physically reachable;
- functional damage from gunfire or explosions;
- immediate cessation of the associated local audio emitter upon disabling damage;
- optional visible breakage, sparks, smoke, displaced parts, falling, or brief static;
- persistent state for the remainder of the loaded encounter unless a mission-specific
  reset is deliberately authored.

CLASS B — EXPOSED INSTALLATION-GRADE DEVICES

Conditionally destructible class.

Examples:

- wall-mounted commercial speakers;
- exposed public-address horns;
- exposed venue speakers;
- visible amplifier racks;
- fixed intercom stations;
- vehicle or vessel loudhailers;
- exposed alarm speakers.

Expected behavior depends on construction and mission needs:

- may require heavier damage than a domestic radio;
- may disable locally without destroying the full network;
- may be protected by grilles, housings, or mounting brackets;
- may be vulnerable to direct fire while upstream audio continues through other nodes;
- should not be treated as indestructible merely because they are fixed in place.

CLASS C — HARDENED, RECESSED, OR CONCEALED AUDIO INFRASTRUCTURE

Default exemption from ordinary small-arms destruction and direct player operation.

Examples:

- reinforced speakers behind armored grilles;
- recessed ceiling or wall speakers whose working components are not exposed;
- concealed speakers integrated into architecture;
- buried, shielded, or inaccessible audio cabling;
- protected connection hardware inside locked or armored service spaces;
- hardened public-address amplifiers and distribution equipment;
- redundant installation hardware whose destruction would require access to a control
  cabinet, power source, network node, or demolition charge rather than a casual shot.

Expected behavior:

- no false interaction prompt on inaccessible hardware;
- no obligation to fail from incidental pistol or rifle fire against the surrounding
  wall or grille;
- local audio may still cease if power, upstream control, network routing, or a mission-
  authored amplifier node is disabled;
- destruction, sabotage, or shutdown remains possible where a controlling source gives
  the player credible access to the relevant hardware.

The exemption protects plausibility. It does not create magical audio. A hardened
speaker can survive nearby gunfire, but it still requires power, signal, and functioning
upstream equipment.

================================================================================
INTERACTION RULES
================================================================================

PROMPT VISIBILITY

Audio-device prompts follow the existing narrative/world action prompt standard:

- the device is in the player's direct line of sight;
- the player is within close physical proximity;
- no physical obstruction prevents the player's hand from reaching the control;
- the device is in a state that supports the proposed action;
- the prompt is world-anchored to the operable part of the device rather than floating
  generically in the room.

DEFAULT FUNCTIONAL WORDING

For ordinary player-operated audio devices, use restrained functional wording:

    TURN OFF

When a mission benefits from reversible operation and the device remains functional:

    TURN ON

The prompt should not be converted into a character-defining metaphor merely because
the broader system supports protagonist-specific wording. Operating a normal radio is
usually a mundane physical action. The wording should remain legible and quiet unless
a mission source establishes a specific narrative reason to depart from it.

INPUT AND ANIMATION

- one press operates the relevant switch, knob, button, remote, or reachable control;
- the hand animation should match the actual device rather than use an abstract generic
  gesture where practical;
- audio response should begin on the physical control action, not before contact;
- no progress bar is required for a simple switch operation;
- if the player interrupts the animation through damage, forced movement, or another
  valid high-priority state, the device remains in whichever state the physical action
  has actually reached.

PROMPT SUPPRESSION

Do not display TURN OFF or TURN ON when:

- the device is already destroyed or functionally disabled;
- its controls are inaccessible or concealed;
- the player is looking through glass, grating, fencing, or another obstruction;
- the local device is only a passive speaker and the actual control is elsewhere;
- mission scripting has removed player control for a justified, explicitly authored
  reason;
- the device is no longer the source of the audible track.

================================================================================
DAMAGE AND AUDIO RESPONSE
================================================================================

FUNCTIONAL DAMAGE STATES

Production may implement the following state model directly or map it into an existing
generic prop-damage framework:

1. INTACT / ON
   - device emits its assigned audio;
   - TURN OFF is available when reachable.

2. INTACT / OFF
   - device remains functional but silent;
   - TURN ON may be available when reversible operation is supported.

3. DAMAGED / FUNCTIONAL
   - optional intermediate state;
   - audio may distort, flutter, lose one channel, change volume, or intermittently cut;
   - the device can still be operated if controls remain usable;
   - use sparingly and only when the asset supports a readable intermediate state.

4. DISABLED
   - functional threshold crossed;
   - local audio emitter stops immediately;
   - interaction prompt is removed unless a repair system is explicitly authored;
   - chassis may remain visually recognizable and largely intact.

5. DESTROYED
   - severe damage, fragmentation, burning, major deformation, or complete physical
     breakup;
   - no audio output and no ordinary operation prompt;
   - any residual electrical tick, spark, capacitor discharge, or dying static must be
     brief and clearly separate from continued program audio.

DIRECT FIRE

- direct projectile impacts apply damage according to calibre, impact energy, material,
  penetration, and the existing prop-damage model;
- a single credible hit to an exposed domestic radio may be enough to disable it even
  if the casing is not theatrically destroyed;
- low-energy grazing hits may produce cosmetic damage without immediate failure;
- repeated hits should not leave the device playing indefinitely after obvious severe
  damage;
- penetration through the device must not leave an invisible duplicate audio emitter
  active at its original location.

EXPLOSIONS AND BLAST

- nearby explosions apply damage and impulse according to distance, shielding, and
  line of effect;
- an explosion can disable the device without directly fragmenting it;
- a device thrown from a surface by blast may stop because of internal damage, cable
  separation, power loss, battery displacement, or impact with the environment;
- occluding walls and substantial furniture may reduce damage and impulse;
- mission-critical audio should not be protected by making the visible source
  invulnerable. If a line must be heard, deliver it through another credible source or
  gate destructibility until the information is no longer required.

AUDIO CUTOFF

When a local device becomes disabled or destroyed:

- stop or rapidly collapse its local program-audio emitter;
- preserve spatial continuity: other radios or speakers carrying the same broadcast
  continue unless they are also disabled or share a genuinely interrupted upstream
  source;
- do not fade cleanly for several seconds if the physical failure is abrupt;
- a sub-second sputter, static burst, tape-stop artifact, speaker cone pop, or electrical
  crackle may punctuate the failure where appropriate;
- dialogue and mission-critical sound remain governed by their own priority mix.

PHYSICS AND STATE PERSISTENCE

- a displaced device carries its emitter with it while still functional;
- audio must not remain fixed at the original placement after the prop falls or moves;
- if power is supplied by a visible cable, cable separation may disable the device;
- if the device is battery powered, displacement alone need not silence it;
- destruction and manual shutdown states persist across nearby checkpoints where the
  encounter state is expected to persist;
- reload behavior must not revive a deliberately destroyed device unless the checkpoint
  restores the entire environment to an earlier authored state.

================================================================================
NETWORKED AND MULTI-SPEAKER SYSTEMS
================================================================================

A visible radio or control unit may feed one or more external speakers. Production must
define which component owns the signal and which components own local emission.

Rules:

- destroying one passive speaker silences that speaker only;
- destroying the source, amplifier, or distribution unit can silence all dependent
  speakers if the connection is physically and logically credible;
- destroying a decorative control panel must not silence unrelated speakers unless it
  actually controls them;
- redundant public-address systems may continue through surviving nodes;
- concealed speaker exemptions do not protect an exposed source unit from damage;
- interaction prompts appear on the reachable control point, not every passive speaker.

================================================================================
AI AND WORLD RESPONSE
================================================================================

Audio-device interaction is primarily environmental, but AI may react when context
supports it.

Possible reactions:

- nearby occupants notice an abrupt shot, explosion, or smashed device;
- a manually switched-off radio is normally quiet enough not to create an alert;
- an NPC may notice that expected music or a broadcast has stopped if a mission source
  explicitly uses that change as a clue;
- combat AI should not prioritize repairing or restarting incidental radios;
- scripted characters should not continue commenting as though a destroyed device is
  still audibly playing.

Do not turn every radio into a stealth alarm puzzle. Reaction must be authored where it
adds value rather than inferred universally.

================================================================================
ACCESSIBILITY AND MIXING
================================================================================

- prompts use the standard remappable interaction input;
- subtitle or music-identification UI, where provided, must update when the source stops;
- players with reduced dynamic range still need a readable difference between active,
  switched-off, and destroyed states;
- essential dialogue must not depend on hearing a destructible incidental device;
- radio music remains below mission-critical dialogue, warnings, and combat readability;
- destruction transients should respect loudness and sensory-accessibility settings.

================================================================================
PERFORMANCE AND AUTHORING GUIDANCE
================================================================================

Use asset tiers rather than unique simulation for every object.

HERO DEVICE

- bespoke interaction animation;
- multiple visual damage states;
- authored audio failure;
- persistent mission consequence;
- named or narratively important placement.

STANDARD INTERACTIVE DEVICE

- generic operate animation;
- one intact and one disabled/destroyed state;
- local audio emitter cutoff;
- ordinary physics and checkpoint persistence.

BACKGROUND DEVICE

- may use a simple damage-to-disable event;
- no bespoke interaction if inaccessible or irrelevant;
- must still avoid visibly playing after obvious destruction.

================================================================================
CONTINUITY CONSTRAINTS
================================================================================

- Do not leave ordinary exposed radios invulnerable by default.
- Do not allow destroyed audio devices to continue clean program playback.
- Do not place operation prompts on inaccessible passive speakers.
- Do not require reinforced, recessed, or concealed audio hardware to fail from casual
  small-arms impacts.
- Do not treat hardened installation hardware as independent of power and signal.
- Do not make every manually switched-off device an AI alert trigger.
- Do not use a prolonged clean fade for an abrupt physical destruction event.
- Do not make mission-critical exposition depend solely on an audio device the player
  can destroy before hearing it.
- Preserve the existing contextual, close-proximity, unobstructed prompt rules.

================================================================================
OPEN PRODUCTION REQUIREMENTS
================================================================================

- map the device classes into the common prop-damage component;
- define default health, material, penetration, blast, and impulse profiles by asset
  family;
- author a reusable local-emitter shutdown interface;
- define source, amplifier, speaker, power, and signal relationships for multi-node
  systems;
- author generic TURN OFF and TURN ON interaction animations;
- establish checkpoint serialization for operation and destruction states;
- define subtitle and track-identification behavior after source interruption;
- validate network replication if cooperative or networked modes are later pursued;
- audit existing missions for exposed audio props that currently lack interaction or
  damage response;
- identify intentional exceptions and record them in the controlling mission source.

RESULT

Project Warriors now has a game-wide physical-consistency rule for ordinary diegetic
audio devices. Exposed radios and similar sound sources can be operated, damaged, and
disabled; their local audio responds immediately and spatially to those state changes.
Reinforced, recessed, concealed, or otherwise protected infrastructure is exempt from
casual destruction but remains dependent on credible power, signal, and upstream
hardware.
