PURPOSE
Document a weapon-heat gameplay layer that discourages habitual reload–magdump–repeat behavior without making automatic fire useless. The system should reward controlled bursts, fire discipline, weapon-role awareness, and deliberate cooling or barrel-management decisions.

AUTHORITY / CONTINUITY
- Later explicit owner adjudication controls.
- Existing malfunction design remains controlling: heat, sustained fire, and suppressor backpressure are plausible risk sources; pristine weapons do not receive an equal random failure chance on every shot.
- Persistent physical weapon state remains controlling. Reloading, switching weapons, dropping a weapon, entering a vehicle, or handing the weapon to another actor does not erase stored heat.
- Exact temperatures, thresholds, timing, durability values, animation lengths, and platform coefficients remain production work.

================================================================================
01. CORE DESIGN LOCK
================================================================================

RECOMMENDED LOCK
Select-fire weapons accumulate thermal load from firing. Sustained automatic fire raises heat much faster than disciplined semi-automatic fire or short bursts. As heat crosses weapon-specific thresholds, the firearm becomes progressively less controllable, less precise, and more prone to heat-related stoppages or severe failure states.

The system exists to discourage constant pray-and-spray or reload–magdump–repeat play. It must still preserve legitimate automatic-fire use:
- close-range emergency action
- breaking an ambush
- short violent room-entry or vehicle-contact windows
- suppression by weapons designed and supplied for that role
- rare authored moments where sustained output is tactically justified

The game should punish abuse of automatic fire, not the existence of automatic fire.

================================================================================
02. THERMAL LOAD, NOT A MAGAZINE COUNTER
================================================================================

RECOMMENDED LOCK
The simulation tracks accumulated thermal load rather than applying a flat penalty for each magazine emptied.

A magazine-based shorthand is too coarse because heat depends on:
- ammunition count and cartridge energy
- cyclic rate
- burst length and spacing
- barrel mass and profile
- operating system and action type
- open-bolt or closed-bolt behavior
- suppressor, muzzle device, and gas setting
- ambient temperature, wind, rain, snow, and water exposure
- whether the barrel, suppressor, receiver, or feed system was already hot

The player may experience the pattern as “each consecutive magdump is worse,” but the underlying state should remain continuous and weapon-specific.

COOLING RULE
Heat decays continuously whenever the weapon is not firing. A longer pause cools more than a brief trigger release. Reloading alone is not a reset.

Transient heat penalties disappear when the relevant components cool below their thresholds. Permanent wear or damage, if implemented, is a separate weapon-condition layer and should not be silently repaired by cooling.

================================================================================
03. PROGRESSIVE HEAT STATES
================================================================================

Exact labels do not need to appear in the HUD. They are design states for implementation.

STATE 0 — NORMAL
- normal accuracy and dispersion for the weapon and shooter state
- normal cyclic behavior
- baseline malfunction risk based on condition, ammunition, environment, and magazine/feed state

STATE 1 — WARM
- subtle barrel/suppressor heat cues
- mild heat mirage through magnified optics where physically relevant
- slightly slower recovery during long automatic strings
- no dramatic hidden penalty

STATE 2 — HOT
- increased mechanical dispersion and degraded practical grouping
- more pronounced muzzle climb and slower recoil recovery during automatic fire
- increased probability of plausible heat-related stoppages
- stronger mirage, smoke, odor, discoloration, or glow cues where appropriate
- suppressor signature and handling consequences become more noticeable

STATE 3 — CRITICAL
- severe practical accuracy degradation during continued fire
- weapon-specific cyclic inconsistency, short-stroking, feed disruption, or reduced reliability may occur
- the player is strongly encouraged to cease fire, transition weapons, cool the system, or change barrels where supported
- severe heat can create platform-specific danger states, including runaway behavior, component failure, or a chambered-round cook-off

STATE 4 — FAILURE / UNSAFE
Rare and difficult to reach outside deliberate abuse, specialist support-weapon use, or authored high-intensity sequences.
- weapon may become temporarily or permanently hard-disabled
- suppressor, barrel, gas-system, handguard, feed, or other component may fail according to platform
- a chambered round may ignite without a normal trigger press on susceptible closed-bolt systems
- the game must not treat every weapon as having the same catastrophic endpoint

================================================================================
04. ACCURACY AND HANDLING CONSEQUENCES
================================================================================

RECOMMENDED LOCK
Heat should not create arcade “random bloom” detached from the weapon. Consequences should be expressed through physical and readable causes:
- barrel and component temperature increasing dispersion
- optic mirage degrading target clarity
- shooter discomfort or altered grip on exposed hot surfaces
- harsher recoil recovery during extended strings
- weapon-specific gas or cycling irregularity

The first rounds of a controlled burst should remain useful. The main loss of precision should emerge when the player insists on holding the trigger or repeatedly resumes fire before the weapon has recovered.

Semi-automatic fire and short bursts should be the most heat-efficient default for rifles, carbines, SMGs, and PDWs. Burst length should remain a tactical choice rather than a universal fixed optimum.

================================================================================
05. MALFUNCTION AND SEVERE-FAILURE INTEGRATION
================================================================================

THERMAL RISK
Heat modifies the existing malfunction-state model; it does not create a separate arcade jam lottery.

At elevated heat, plausible outcomes may include:
- failure to feed or return fully into battery
- extraction/ejection problems
- magazine, belt, link, or feed-cover disruption on support weapons
- gas-system or cycling inconsistency
- suppressor- or ammunition-related backpressure problems
- platform-specific component failure

COOK-OFF RULE
Cook-off is an extreme, platform-specific consequence, not a common generic penalty.
- most relevant when a live round remains chambered in a severely overheated closed-bolt weapon
- may cause an unintended discharge even without a trigger press
- direction follows the actual muzzle orientation
- safety, selector position, and trigger discipline do not guarantee prevention once the cartridge is heat-ignited
- open-bolt support weapons reduce this particular risk by not normally leaving a round chambered between bursts, though they remain vulnerable to other heat failures

RATE-OF-FIRE RULE
A dropped or inconsistent cyclic rate is allowed only where the platform and failure mode support it. Do not apply one universal “slow fire” penalty to all hot weapons.

NPC PARITY
Allied and enemy weapons should obey the same broad heat logic where simulation scope permits. Low-discipline enemies may overfire and create their own stoppages. Trained troops and elite enemies should use burst discipline, rotate weapons, change barrels, or break contact before reaching critical heat.

================================================================================
06. SUPPRESSOR INTERACTION
================================================================================

RECOMMENDED LOCK
Suppressors accelerate and retain thermal load during sustained fire. They should make prolonged automatic fire materially less sustainable.

POSSIBLE CONSEQUENCES
- faster transition into HOT and CRITICAL states
- stronger heat mirage through optics
- visible glow in low light at severe temperatures
- increased backpressure-related fouling and cycling stress on relevant platforms
- temporary handling hazard during attachment changes
- suppressor degradation or failure under extreme abuse where supported by the specific device

A suppressor is not a universal reliability penalty during ordinary disciplined fire. The exacerbation should become meaningful when the player uses sustained automatic fire, unsuitable ammunition/settings, or an already overheated system.

Low-light stealth consequence:
A glowing or smoking suppressor can reveal recent firing and weaken the player’s visual concealment even when muzzle flash remains reduced.

================================================================================
07. MACHINE GUNS AND SUPPORT WEAPONS
================================================================================

ROLE DISTINCTION
Light, medium, and heavy machine guns are designed to sustain more automatic fire than ordinary rifles, carbines, SMGs, or PDWs. They should receive:
- higher thermal capacity where appropriate
- heavier barrel profiles
- open-bolt operation where applicable
- more forgiving sustained-fire doctrine
- weapon-specific feed and barrel-management logic

They are not immune to heat.

QUICK-CHANGE BARREL MECHANIC
Appropriate machine guns may support an in-game barrel change when:
- the weapon actually has a quick-change or crew-changeable barrel
- a compatible spare barrel is carried or available
- the player is in a physically credible position to perform the action
- the current barrel is not blocked by damage or geometry

The barrel change should be a vulnerable authored interaction, not an instant cooldown button.

Possible prompts:
- CHANGE BARREL
- ORDER BARREL CHANGE
- HOLD FIRE
- COOL WEAPON

BARREL-CHANGE STATE EFFECT
Changing the barrel greatly reduces barrel-specific heat and restores much of the weapon’s sustained-fire capacity. It does not automatically cool:
- receiver
- chamber/feed area
- gas system
- suppressor or muzzle device
- ammunition box/belt area
- surrounding components already heat-soaked

The newly installed barrel begins at its own stored temperature. A recently removed barrel remains dangerously hot and cannot be treated as ordinary inventory.

CREW / SQUAD SUPPORT
Some weapons may require or strongly benefit from an assistant gunner carrying the spare barrel, ammunition, heat-resistant equipment, or helping cover the change. This creates a squad-logistics distinction rather than giving every protagonist a universal spare barrel.

================================================================================
08. PLAYER FEEDBACK AND UI
================================================================================

RECOMMENDED LOCK
The system should be readable primarily through weapon behavior, animation, audio, and visible condition.

DIEGETIC CUES
- increasing heat mirage
- smoke, odor, discoloration, or glow where appropriate
- changing mechanical cadence
- hand repositioning or avoidance of hot surfaces
- character bark or squad warning used sparingly
- inspect animation revealing heat and condition cues
- barrel/suppressor handling restrictions

STANDARD DIFFICULTY
May use a restrained contextual indication such as:
- WEAPON HOT
- HOLD FIRE
- CHANGE BARREL

No large arcade heat bar is required by default.

MAXIMUM REALISM
Relies primarily on physical cues, weapon knowledge, cadence, and inspection. Optional accessibility settings may expose a clearer thermal indicator without changing the underlying simulation.

================================================================================
09. DIFFICULTY AND BALANCE
================================================================================

LOWER DIFFICULTIES
- slower heat accumulation
- faster cooling
- softer accuracy and malfunction consequences
- clearer warning cues
- catastrophic states strongly suppressed outside authored moments

HIGHER / MAXIMUM REALISM
- platform-authentic accumulation and cooling relationships
- stronger suppressor and environment interaction
- fewer explicit warnings
- more meaningful stoppage and barrel-management consequences
- thermal state persists through weapon switching, reloads, mounting, and handoffs

Balance must preserve fun and readability. The mechanic should reward knowledge and discipline, not require memorizing hidden temperature values.
